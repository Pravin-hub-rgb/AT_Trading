# Trading Entry Methods, Part 2: Aggressive Entries, Reversals & Panic Selling

This is the second part of the entry-method series. In the last video we discussed how many entry types exist and went into detail on the aggressive entry. To recap, there are three entry methods: the conservative method, the mid-conservative method, and the aggressive method.

The more confirmation we wait for, the more momentum we miss in the market — that's a fact everyone knows. The more you wait for confirmation before executing, the more momentum you lose, because the momentum is already being consumed while you wait for confirmation. That's why the most preferable entry choices are the mid-conservative and the aggressive ones. If you look at my style of execution, I use the aggressive one — the aggressive entry method is what I take.

In the last video we saw how, in the aggressive method, we first build the major overview and, according to it, execute the aggressive entry on the current day if confirmation comes. So the aggressive entry is built according to the major overview. But wait — just because you've created an overview, is it necessary that the market will move according to your overview? The answer is no. Some of the time — or even most of the time — the general probability of a trade continuing is about 60%. So if you define a major overview, about 60% of the time the overview is right at the beginning itself — the probability of the trade continuing is 60%. For anyone watching this video, the probability of the trend continuing is roughly 60%. So the overview will automatically be right 60% of the time.

But that doesn't mean the overview is right 60% of the time and we still lose. Then why do we make losses? Because even though the overview is right and the trend continues 60% of the time, the way we enter and exit is wrong — the stop loss gets hit too often, or the risk management is wrong, and that's what causes the losses. But if you look at the trade on a continuing basis, the average ratio is always around 60%. So the overview will be right about 60% of the time — but when it's not right, what do we do then?

That's what we'll discuss in today's video: we take the aggressive entry according to the overview, but we all know how the market is — the market will reverse, and in many cases it will not perform according to our overview. So today's video is an important one, where we'll see how to identify the cases where it won't perform — how to identify that reversal point, or where the market is reversing into panic buying or panic selling. I've said in many videos that the world runs on panic buying and panic selling. So today we'll see how to identify, first, when panic selling is actually reversing.
