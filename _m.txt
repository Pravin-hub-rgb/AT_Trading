exp_where_to_look.py printed times 5h30m earlier than the chart
Told the reader to open 2024-11-17 23:00. There is no trade there. The trade the
script meant is 2024-11-18 04:30, which is exactly what the reader saw on screen and
what they had already quoted back to me.
Cause: I printed datetime.fromtimestamp(entry_ts, utc). entry_ts is a true UTC
epoch. entry_date on the same trade is IST wall-clock, and the chart renders
entry_date. Converting the epoch to a UTC string therefore lands 5h30m behind
the bar the reader is looking at. This is the same trap the C6 comment in
btc_engine.py warns about, in the opposite direction.
Fixed by printing entry_date - the field the chart actually draws - and keeping
the UTC conversion alongside it in brackets so a mismatch shows up instead of
hiding.
The corrected dates, all of which the reader can now find on the chart:
  2024-11-18 04:30  LONG   89296.08 vs 88731.68   +36.97 -> +9.99   (-26.98)
  2023-06-20 21:00  LONG   26721.07 vs 26641.27   +27.01 -> +16.97  (-10.04)
  2024-01-18 13:30  SHORT  42921.47 vs 42932.07   +50.85 -> +42.36   (-8.49)
  2023-03-16 12:15  LONG   24122.22 vs 24411.22    +5.54 -> -1.00   ( -6.54)
Note 2023-06-20 is +5h30m, not exactly 5.30 - close enough to be read as a rounding
mistake and wrong enough to send someone to an empty chart. The earlier
answer also said 2024-11-17 23:00 was the first date to open, and the first
question in this round was about 18 Nov 04:30, so the reader already had the right
bar in front of them and I talked them out of it.
