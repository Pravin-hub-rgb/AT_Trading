exp_sl_origin.py: the shipped causal SL reads the finished leg low
Reading the chart, the user asked whether the causal stop is really causal. On the
18 Nov 2024 04:30 long it lands on the 03:30 candle low, and 03:30 is where the
PREVIOUS swing ended. The forming swing started at 03:45, so the lowest price a
live trader could have used is the 04:00 candle low.
Measured:

  RETRO   sig["swing_start_px"]              89300.10
  CAUSAL  _csl[bar] (shipped)               88735.70   <- low at 03:30
  FORMING running low from 03:45            89300.10   <- low at 04:00

The shipped causal stop is 564 points lower than the forming-swing low. It is
reading a pivot from a leg that had already finished. That is the retroactive
SL one swing earlier, not a different rule.

Whole book, IS, all with exp_causal_sl=1:
  causal as shipped (reset to seg end)   n=494   net=+247.96R  {sl:189, trail:305}
  causal from FORMING swing low          n=496   net=+255.20R  {sl:223, trail:273}
  RETRO for reference                    n=500   net=+325.78R  {sl:187, trail:313}

So the rule the user proposed is worth +7.24R over the shipped one - it is better,
and it is the one that is actually causal. But it does not close the gap to
retro: 255.20 against 325.78 is 70.58R still unexplained. Fixing this is worth
7R of the 78R, not all of it, so something else is also carrying advantage.

257 of 484 shared trades get a different stop under the two causal rules, so this
is not a rare edge case - it touches half the book. 227 are identical.

Both books still exit 18 Nov on 24 Nov 01:45. The stop is hit either way; what
differs is how much R accrued before it was hit, which is exactly the shape of a
stop-placement advantage rather than a different trade.
