# 1 — Overview + Bias Creation (Initial Bias · Pre-Open)

> **Scope:** Everything in this file happens BEFORE the market opens. Purpose: read the market's recent behavior + where price currently sits → declare today's **INITIAL BIAS**: **BUYING / SELLING / NEUTRAL (no-bias)**.
>
> **Deliberately OUT of scope (separate concepts):**
> - Post-open reading (open → ~10 AM morning behavior)
> - Resolution of a NEUTRAL case into a live directional bias ("live bias" step)
> - The entire entry/execution layer (pattern breaks, rejections, entries, stop-losses)
> - Intraday bias persistence / flip arbitration during the live session
>
> **Single source:** `6 days course transcript\clean notes final` (Sessions 1–6). Tags: (S1)…(S6).

---

## 1. Foundation

- **Price above everything.** Decisions are made on price alone — price carries the highest weightage; volume / option strength / order-flow are at most secondary confirmations. When confirmations go AGAINST price, execution still follows price (S1).
- **The market never moves in one direction.** Uptrend → resistance → weakness → correction → fresh buyers → higher. Downtrend mirrored. This single fact is the foundation of everything (S1).
- **Only two kinds of trading days exist:** continuation or reversal. Nothing else gets created (S1).
- **Price has a LIMITED set of possibilities.** Only two participants (buyer/seller) and two activities exist. After any swing, price either proceeds toward the swing's bottom level, or holds higher and proceeds further up. Pre-analyzing every scenario removes the "what is happening / where is it going?" feeling — when one possibility starts printing, you already know what it is and how far it can travel. Nothing outside the limited set can occur (S3).
- **Overview correctness drives everything downstream:** the more consistently your overall read of the day/market is correct, the higher the probability entries and targets play out — execution flows downstream of getting the overview right (S1). Confidence itself is sourced from having the overview right (S6).
- **The workflow order is fixed:** build overview → plan direction → react to the intraday swing INSIDE that plan. The overview protects you (S4). Clarity of decision comes first — knowing from the overview that the market won't fall continuously lets you wait calmly for support confirmation (S2).
- **Role split:** the overview supplies the DIRECTIONAL BIAS; the morning swing supplies the specific entry point (separate step). Morning view = direction; major overview = 3–4-day context (S3, S5).
- **Clubbing:** combine what the overview says with what confirmation shows — together they form the trade plan; this saves wrong trades, reduces trade count, pushes probability higher (S4).
- **Confirmation gate:** secondary confirmations (divergence, option strength) may redirect the planned direction ONLY when they ALSO turn negative AND the overview matches that direction; otherwise price rules (S5).
- The overview even decides the option SIDE (buying vs selling route) before any execution discussion (S2).

## 2. What the Overview Is

- Definition: observing, AT A LARGE LEVEL, what is happening in the market (S2).
- Chart preference: daily timeframe works; his personal choice = **5-minute charts zoomed out** — same data as daily candles but more detail (S2).
- Data requirement: **at least 6–10 trading sessions visible** on the zoomed-out chart (S2).
- The core question: **WHERE is the market trading within its major levels** — running at a high level, performing at a lower level, or stuck inside a range? (S2).
- This is Leg 1 of the system: context from where the market sits inside the major range (higher level / lower level / all-time high / support area) (S3).
- Extension (context only): the same overview method scales to swing frames — major flow/trend identified on the DAILY chart, significant retracements located on HOURLY (S6).

## 3. Building the Overview — the Marking System

### Two-range marking
1. **Recent range** — current/nearest box, generally smaller, marked from recent swing highs/lows (S2).
2. **Next range** — support/resistance marked from the next bigger, older swings (S2).

- ALL range/S-R marking is done ON THE BASIS OF BUYING/SELLING SWINGS (S2).
- Purpose of the two ranges: they tell you WHICH LEVEL the market is actually performing from on a given day (S2).

### Mountain-pattern rule
One big swing fully reversing within a single session (up then all the way back down) = mountain → consider its **MID-POINT** a candidate mark; intermediate points may also be marked if continuation occurs from either side (S2).

### Range update rule
While price stays inside the smaller range, the overview is based on that range. Once it hits an all-time high or breaks any level → the **NEW range replaces the smaller range** and the overview shifts.
Cycle: mark → observe within → level breaks → update → re-observe (S2).

### Reading the big swings
Swings exist at every scale — intraday, weekly, zoomed-out big swings — same rules, universal across timeframes (S2):
- Swing marked start-to-end, whatever the pace.
- **High pace:** fewer candles, continuous movement without retracement, sharp domination. **Low pace:** more candles, retracements along the way.
- Reversals/continuations trigger on a **PACE-OF-MOMENTUM BREAK**, not merely highs/lows breaking.

### Context rules
- Weekly highs count like ATHs for overview purposes (S4).
- Supply-demand logic beneath every level: falling all the way TO a level = supply dominated there (nobody was buying); rising on higher lows = demand exceeding supply. Price does not jump from a major low straight to a major high (S2).

## 4. Previous-Day Close Reads (today's bias inputs)

The whole base method anchors to the **previous day's close** + the first swing forming around that settlement (S2).

- **12:00–2:00 PM holding read:** if the market fell and HOLDS selling pressure into 12–2 = negative close → next day (today) can test LOWER levels. If it rose and holds buying into 12–2 = positive close → today can test HIGHER levels. Strong momentum can extend slightly past 2 PM (S1).
- **Second-half tiebreaker (if confused about the close):** closing toward the HIGHER end of the range in the second half = positive close (good buying-side momentum), and vice versa (S1).
- **Strong-swing override:** if a strong buying swing clearly formed, the session counts positive overall even if things turn slightly negative afterward (mirrored for selling) (S1).
- **Full-session reversal persistence:** yesterday printed selling pressure / lower high / sell-off BUT the entire selling reversed into a buying close ⇒ today's bias REMAINS BUYING despite the choppy sideways tape (S6 — 11th October case; also: expect no continuous retracement at highs).
- **Post-trending-day note:** the session AFTER a one-sided trending session is among the EASIEST to read (S6).
- ⚠️ **DON'T CONFUSE two concepts:** swing STRENGTH/WEAKNESS observation ≠ positive/negative CLOSE overview. A positive close and a weak swing coexist — two different layers (S3).

## 5. Overview Laws (fixed tendencies used to judge every case)

1. At ALL-TIME HIGH / higher level → the market will NOT fall continuously. Support forms first, then bounce; retracement from highs happens in steps (S2, S4).
2. At LOWER LEVEL / support → it will NOT rise continuously. Resistance forms first, then pullback; recovery from lows happens in steps (S2, S4).
3. Continuous reversal is NOT expected from either extreme. Swings are the market's nature — take positions IN LINE with expected momentum, never against it (S2).
4. **One-line law:** HIGHER level test = STRENGTH. LOWER level test = WEAKNESS (S2).
5. When the market arrives at an extreme, exactly TWO outcomes exist: bounce back (or rejection from resistance), or continue (with lower highs / higher lows). It cannot stay in the middle (S2).
6. BIG swings shrink reversal probability — the larger the one-directional momentum, the lower the odds of full reversal back to the other extreme (S5). Mirror rule: an OVERSIZED counter-swing makes the expected continuation-break likely to FAIL (S6 — 13th October nuance).
7. SIZE itself is a strength indicator — a big swing means domination extended far beyond usual; a small swing with retracement = weakness (S3).
8. Once a level is BUILT, the market does NOT come back to retest it in maximum cases (S4).
9. Exhaustion exists even inside strong trends — a strong close plus continuous upside does NOT guarantee continuation; profit booking causes retracement (S3).
10. ANY day's first swing never runs continuously — the bounce back WILL come; continuation afterward isn't guaranteed (S6).
11. Flows end in steps too: counter-swings after gap-ups from highs last ~5/8/10 candles before support; 3–6 day selling flows die into sideways closes at lows. Every weekly-high/all-time-high context resolves via CONTINUATION-or-REVERSAL observation (S6).
12. Choppy mixed-direction days are outside the studied set — learn from days moving in ONE clear flow (S3).

## 6. INITIAL BIAS OUTPUT (the pre-open decision)

### 6.1 BUYING bias — triggers

- **Highs-and-falling rule:** market sitting at MAJOR HIGHS / ATH and falling → BUYING stance — expect support formation and bounce; it will not fall continuously (S3 two-point framework #2; S2; S4). Recorded example: opened into higher levels with buying overview → deep fall unexpected at highs; stance stayed buying even when price dropped toward lows (S6 — 27th Feb).
- **Trend-structure rule:** buying trend intact (HH + HL) → retracement comes → support forms → BUY per overview (S3 trend-following version).
- **Falling-market tendency:** whenever the market is falling, think BUYING FIRST — support exists somewhere, then up (S3 general tendency).
- **ATH + sideways-expiry case:** market near ATH, sideways expiry close, THEN falls → buying overview stands; won't fall continuously — support + good bounce expected, even if full reversal takes multiple sessions and dips repeat (S4).
- **Multi-day flow reversal case:** 3–6 days of selling flow ending in a sideways close AT THE LOWS (weekly/all-time low) → REVERSAL context → buying swing expected next day; minimum expectation = high breakout AND continuation (S6 mirror-flow universe).
- **Strong-flow context:** consecutive prior-day high breakouts = strong buying flow — dips are for buying lower levels, not shorting (S6 best-opportunity doctrine: best opportunity in a buying market at ATHs = a dip giving LOWER levels to buy, regardless of which swing delivers it).
- **Best-case variant (recognizable pre-open):** retracement COMPLETED the previous day + fall resumes next morning → "no better [buying] case exists" (S6 superior variant).
- **Prior-day positive close** → today can test HIGHER levels (S1).
- Practice note: the bias is declared IN ADVANCE from the overview — e.g., day-before declaration "buying flow; retrace → buy at support," then executed accordingly next session (S5 prior-day pair narrative).

### 6.2 SELLING bias — triggers

- **Lows-and-falling rule:** market at MAJOR LOWS / support area and falling → SELLING stance — short on resistance formation (S3 two-point framework #1).
- **Trend-structure rule:** selling trend intact (LL + LH) → pullback comes → resistance forms → SHORT per overview (S3 trend-following version).
- **Rising-market tendency:** whenever the market is rising, think RESISTANCE-then-retrace FIRST (S3 general tendency).
- **Post-one-sided-selling-close:** the day after a heavy one-way selling session → continuation stance — sell resistance, low-breakdown expectation (S6 lower-levels day).
- **Prior-day negative close** → today can test LOWER levels (S1).
- **Default no-short zones (VETO):** at ATH / higher levels we do NOT carry a selling bias — even if a buying swing reversed; weekly highs count like ATHs. Not an absolute ban — an exception exists only through the live MORNING case (separate concept); the overview then governs the next structural entry (S4).
- **Gap veto (checked at open):** flow buying + no lower high formed + prior close at highs + big GAP-DOWN ⇒ the gap ALREADY COUNTS AS THE RETRACEMENT → selling stance INVALID, skipped (S6 classification mini-case).
- Related: a buying swing created after a gap-down is still bought PER FLOW — the gap itself does not flip the bias (S6 gap-down handling).

### 6.3 NEUTRAL (un-biased) — identification ONLY

**Definition:** an indecision market — movement possible BOTH ways, NO bias held (S6).

**How it forms (the recorded case):** market near highs favored a buying read (a sell-off after a lower high carries little continuous momentum → support bounce generally expected) — BUT the structure completed as:
ATH made → sell-off → support held → LOWER HIGH → selling arrived → day CLOSED ON selling-side support ⇒ **indecision** (after a lower high it can fall below; with strength instead it would bounce, make a higher low, continue) (S6).

**Unbiased-day checklist (ask in order):**
1. ATH made?
2. Retested?
3. Support formed/held?
4. Bounce → resistance → sell-off?

All YES ⇒ **indecision market — NO bias** (S6).

Also flaggable as unbiased: a lower high printing into a higher-level range with no resolution — guidance recorded: the NEXT day decides (S5 — 27th Feb recap).

**Handling:** when neutral is identified, resolution happens through LIVE action after open — that is the separate **"live bias" concept, out of scope here** (S2, S5, S6).

## 7. Pre-Open Judgment Calls (conflict & coexistence)

- **Two overviews can COEXIST** (e.g., ATH-falling buy-support read vs recent-high rejection short). If genuinely confused between them → SKIP; don't force a bias. Confused? Skip (S4 — 20th Feb).
- **Never flip sides in confusion** — buy/sell/buy switching ruins accounts; act only after momentum arrives and congestion breaks proving one side dominates (arbitration completes live) (S4).
- **Overview falsified:** if price travels all the way to the opposite intraday extreme, the overview was simply wrong that day (S2 SL corollary).
