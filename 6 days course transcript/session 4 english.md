Good evening, everyone. Today's session is about **confirmation** and the market's behavior — following on from what we've discussed over the last few days.

## Confirmation — why the challenge

The challenge comes when you try to **find out the resistance at the moment it is being formed**. That is a little difficult — you have to understand the concept fully before you can call it early. `[unclear — some lines here]`

The core concept: if a **buying swing** is forming, you wait. Once the buying swing completes and a **reversal** happens, that acts as the confirmation — the market has given its view on the buying side. We don't mix setups: if the buying view reverses against us, we react according to the overview, not by improvising.

## Overview before reaction

The **major overview** tells you what to do even when the market's first move goes against you:

- If the market is in a **buying overview**, then even if it dips and looks like a selling swing forms, you wait for support to hold, then you buy.
- If your overview is selling, you won't suddenly flip to buying just because of a short-lived buying swing. **The overview protects you.**

So the sequence always goes: **build the overview → plan the direction → then react to the actual intraday swing within that plan.**

### The "10%" exception
He noted a smaller probability case (maybe ~10% of days): the market opens, moves, then drops, and only later confirms the buying strength (an inverted view). In such a case you still proceed according to the final overview once buying strength is confirmed.

## Domination & repetition ("double domination")

He introduced a **domination** idea: the market keeps showing which side is dominating. For example:
- If the market makes a selling swing but can't **sustain** it, and buyers keep buying at higher levels — then after a retracement the market may go up again.
- But if **sellers repeatedly dominate** (double domination of sellers), the probability shifts — the market's selling-side continuation becomes more likely.

So the market's own repetition becomes a signal: when a side keeps getting dominant over and over, the continuation probability of that side rises. This is more of a **conceptual observation** (part of reading market nature) than a standalone "trading system."

## The losing/ROI side & why selling

Confirmed (confirmatory) entries produce a **high hit rate**, but the trade duration is longer and the stop bigger, so the overall ROI may be lower. That's fine — it suits a beginner.

He also discussed **capital / ROI**:
- With a small SL you may get a ~1:3 reward ratio; wins are small, but stable, and your risk/reward stays favourable; if you do lose, the loss is small.
- You can't always deploy a huge capital in the same option (e.g., taking 200–300-rupee premiums) because you won't get good pricing in a small range. So the big-capital trader can't easily keep the same return ratio without limits; but with small capital the % return is high.
- He trades **monthly option selling**, which he finds the best fit: with a bigger stop, the trade is held longer; executing via **option selling** is better because the setup lines up with a bigger stop and a more comfortable hold.
