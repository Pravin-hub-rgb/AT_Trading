# Overview Theory — 6-Day Session Summary

This file is the working theory for building a proper market overview from the 6 English sessions.

The core idea is:
- the market has limited possibilities
- price keeps creating swings
- after each swing, the market usually either goes toward the swing low or moves higher again
- the job is to read where the market is relative to recent swing highs/lows and decide the likely plan

This is not random guessing. It is probability-based reading of structure, momentum, and support/resistance.

---

## 1) What is an overview?

Overview = the market's big-picture condition at the moment.

It answers:
- Is the market in a buying environment or selling environment?
- Is it near a recent high or recent low?
- Is it trading inside a range or breaking structure?
- Which side is more probable right now?

The overview is built before the entry.

The actual entry happens only after the market gives confirmation.

---

## 2) Core market rule from all 6 sessions

The market always behaves in limited ways.

Common market nature:
- open, then move up or down
- after a move, it may retrace
- sometimes it makes higher highs, sometimes lower highs
- sometimes it makes higher lows, sometimes lower lows
- price can only do limited set of actions in a session

So the trader's job is not to ask:
- "Where exactly will it go today?"

Instead the job is:
- "What is the most probable direction given current structure?"
- "Where is the likely support or resistance?"
- "Which side has stronger probability right now?"

This reduces fear and gives execution confidence.

---

## 3) The big idea: recent swing highs and lows

This was repeated several times in the sessions.

The first thing to look at is the recent swing structure.

Ask:
- What is the recent swing high?
- What is the recent swing low?
- Is the market trading near the swing high or near the swing low?
- Is it making higher highs or lower highs?
- Is it making higher lows or lower lows?

This tells you where the market is relative to the recent range.

### Simple logic
- If the market is near a recent high:
  - there is a chance of resistance
  - the market may struggle to keep going up endlessly
  - price often forms resistance first, then retraces
  - the default expectation is not “buy blindly at the top”

- If the market is near a recent low:
  - there is a chance of support
  - price often bounces there
  - the default expectation is not “sell blindly at the bottom”

This is the base of overview formation.

Important note:
- being near a recent high does not automatically mean “sell now”
- being near a recent low does not automatically mean “buy now”
- the important part is whether the market is rejecting the level or accepting it

---

## 4) The market should be read as a sequence

The sessions kept repeating this chain:

1. See the major market condition
2. Find the recent swing high/low
3. Observe the current price relative to those levels
4. Decide whether the market is in buying or selling context
5. Identify support/resistance around that context
6. Wait for confirmation
7. Enter in the direction of the higher probability

This is the real overview method.

Not:
- single candle reaction
- random setup hunting
- entering on emotion

But:
- context first
- structure second
- confirmation third

---

## 5) The overview is not just “buying or selling”

The market can be:
- bullish overview
- bearish overview
- neutral/uncertain overview
- range-bound overview

### Bullish overview
Usually means:
- market is holding above support
- buyers are defending lower levels
- recent lows are being respected
- a dip toward support is a possible buying area

The trader's plan becomes:
- buy near support
- wait for confirmation of demand
- prefer buying the pullback, not the top

### Bearish overview
Usually means:
- market is failing at highs
- sellers are defending resistance
- recent highs are being rejected
- a move toward resistance is a possible selling area

The trader's plan becomes:
- sell near resistance
- wait for rejection or failure to continue upward
- prefer selling the pullback into supply, not the bottom

### Neutral overview
Usually means:
- the market is chopping
- no clear directional dominance
- no clean support or resistance confirmation

The trader's plan becomes:
- wait
- do not force trades
- follow a clean dominant swing when it appears

---

## 6) The rule: "buying overview near support / selling overview near resistance"

This is the most practical rule from the sessions.

### General tendency
- Falling market -> first thought should be buying
  - because it will often find support
  - it won't keep falling endlessly without a bounce

- Rising market -> first thought should be resistance then retrace
  - because it will often form resistance and reverse or pull back

This is a tendency, not a certainty.

### So the plan is:
- In a falling market, look for support and buy only if support holds
- In a rising market, look for resistance and sell only if resistance rejects

This is the overview-based trade setup.

---

## 7) The role of support and resistance

Support and resistance are not mystical levels.

They are simply places where market behavior changes.

### Support
- area where buyers are stepping in
- demand zone
- a place where the market often bounces

### Resistance
- area where sellers are stepping in
- supply zone
- a place where the market often rejects

### Main idea
- Buy around support when the overview supports it
- Sell around resistance when the overview supports it

Do not buy at the very top just because the market is near a high.
Do not sell at the very bottom just because the market is near a low.

Wait for the level to show acceptance or rejection.

### Correct interpretation of “near highs / near lows”
This is the most common confusion, so make it explicit:

- If price is near a recent swing high, the market is usually close to a likely **resistance zone**. The default tendency is not to buy blindly at the top; it is to look for rejection or selling pressure.
- If price is near a recent swing low, the market is usually close to a likely **support zone**. The default tendency is not to sell blindly at the bottom; it is to look for support hold or buying pressure.
- The correct overview is not: “top means buy because it cannot fall from there.”
- The correct overview is: “top means watch for resistance; bottom means watch for support; only trade when the level accepts or rejects cleanly.”

This is the exact setup logic the sessions kept repeating.

## 8) Why the market has limited possibilities

From the sessions:
- market is time-limited
- activity is limited by session timing
- types of swings are limited
- after a swing forms, price usually goes either toward lower support or higher continuation

This gives confidence.

Because if you know the limited possibilities, you don't panic.

You think in terms like:
- this swing could either continue or reverse
- if it rejects the level, the probability is against the move
- if it holds the level, the probability moves in the direction of continuation

This is the reason why a well-built overview gives confidence in execution.

## 9) What is a swing?

A swing is a directional move of candles that creates a structure.

The sessions discussed that the key is not a single candle.

It is the pattern of movement.

### Examples of swing logic
- a buying swing can be strong or weak
- a selling swing can be strong or weak
- strength = momentum keeps pushing in one direction
- weakness = market keeps retracing and fails to sustain

### Practical rule
- strong buying swing = demand is stronger, continuation probability increases
- weak buying swing = retrace or reversal more likely
- strong selling swing = supply is stronger, continuation probability increases
- weak selling swing = bounce or reversal more likely

The trader therefore uses the swing to decide whether the market is likely to continue or reverse.

---

## 10) Strength vs weakness and the overview

This is a huge part of the sessions.

A swing can be:
- strong continuation
- weak continuation
- reversal ready

### Key rule
- If the first swing is weak, you expect more retracement and weaker continuation
- If the first swing is strong, you expect continuation or strong follow-through

This helps you decide your plan.

Example:
- weak selling swing + support formed = recovery / intraday high breakout possibility
- weak buying swing + resistance formed = reversal or intraday low breakdown possibility

The overview is formed by combining:
- major market view
- swing strength/weakness
- current price relative to support/resistance

---

## 11) The two-entry and two-leg concept

The instructors kept saying you should not force too many entries.

### Rule
- maximum two entries on a day in the same setup
- if the first trade works, do not force a second one unless the setup still aligns
- if the first entry fails, do not keep turning a losing idea into another trade just because you feel desperate

This is about discipline.

### Why it matters
The market often gives only a few good setups on a good day.
The trader's job is not to be in every move.
The job is to wait for the right move and keep risk controlled.

## 12) What was the actual "overview" method in practice?

From the sessions, the overview building process was roughly:

### Step A — identify the major range
Look at recent swing highs and lows.
Ask:
- Is the market near a major high?
- Is it near a major low?
- Is it trading in a wider range?

### Step B — identify current position within range
- above recent range = bullish bias
- below recent range = bearish bias
- mid-range = neutral / wait

### Step C — decide likely reaction zone
- near highs -> resistance likely
- near lows -> support likely

### Step D — decide the first plan
- In higher context: look for selling on resistance / failed continuation
- In lower context: look for buying on support / failed breakdown

### Step E — wait for confirmation
- does the level reject?
- does the market hold support?
- does momentum show up?

### Step F — enter only with confirmation
- not before the pattern completes
- not just because you “feel” a move is coming
- not because of one candle alone

## 13) The cleanest overview logic

This is the practical summary:

### If market is trading near recent highs
Likely overview:
- sellers may be active
- resistance is being tested or formed
- the market may be weak above the zone

Trading plan:
- look for resistance rejection
- sell only if the resistance rejects and confirms
- if support is still far, be cautious

### If market is trading near recent lows
Likely overview:
- buyers may be active
- support is being tested or formed
- the market may be weak below the zone

Trading plan:
- look for support hold
- buy only if support accepts and confirms
- if resistance is still far, be patient

### If market is making higher highs and higher lows
Likely overview:
- buying context is stronger
- pullbacks can be buying opportunities

Trading plan:
- buy on retracement toward support
- don't force short just because price is a little high

### If market is making lower lows and lower highs
Likely overview:
- selling context is stronger
- rallies can be selling opportunities

Trading plan:
- sell on bounce toward resistance
- don't force buy just because price is a little low

## 14) The most important caution

This is a very important correction for the user's memory:

If you are near highs, it does not automatically mean “buy, because price cannot fall straight from the top.”

The more accurate statement is:
- near highs, the market is often in a position where it may form resistance and then retrace or reverse
- buying is usually only done when support holds or the market is still in a bullish structure and a pullback is offering a valid buy setup

Likewise:
- near lows, the market is often in a position where it may form support and then recover
- selling is usually only done when resistance rejects or the bearish structure continues

The overview is not a single position.
It is a probability-weighted current market environment.

---

## 15) The overview rules in one sentence

A simple rule set:
- first see where the market stands relative to recent swing highs/lows
- decide whether the current environment is buying, selling, or neutral
- then plan the trade around support or resistance
- wait for confirmation before entering
- trade the highest-probability side, not the most emotional side

---

## 16) A practical checklist for building overview

Use this checklist before taking any trade:

1. What is the recent swing high?
2. What is the recent swing low?
3. Is price closer to the swing high or swing low?
4. Is the market making higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows?
5. What is the major direction of the recent structure?
6. Is the market in a bullish, bearish, or neutral state?
7. Where is the likely support zone?
8. Where is the likely resistance zone?
9. Is the level holding or rejecting?
10. Is there confirmation from momentum or rejection?
11. Is this a continuation or reversal setup?
12. Do I have a clean, small-risk plan?

If you cannot answer these clearly, the overview is weak.
When the overview is weak, wait.

---

## 17) How to plan a trade from overview

Once overview is built, the trade plan becomes simple:

### Buying plan
- market is in buying context
- price is near support or a higher low area
- support is holding
- buyers are defending
- entry triggers after confirmation

### Selling plan
- market is in selling context
- price is near resistance or lower high area
- resistance is rejecting
- sellers are defending
- entry triggers after confirmation

### No-trade plan
- market is mixed or uncertain
- structure is unclear
- support/resistance is not behaving cleanly
- no confirmation from swing or momentum
