## Basic market truth: nothing moves in a straight line forever

Nobody has ever seen a market go up and just keep going up forever without any pullback. If the market is going to go up, how will it get there? Maybe for a day or two it looks like a straight line, but eventually — even across just a couple of trading sessions — once it opens and starts moving up, weakness will show up at some point, and the market will correct. If it's going to go higher after that, it corrects first, then resumes.

## Only two kinds of trading days

So here's the core simplification: there are really only two types of days —

1. **Continuation** — the market keeps going in the direction it started.
2. **Reversal** — the market flips direction instead.

Whether it's Indian markets or any market, going by how we structure our timing, there are exactly these two kinds of trading sessions, and these are the two we need to learn to trade. Either the day becomes a continuation case, or a reversal case. Nothing else gets created outside of these two.

## The core method: waiting for "10 o'clock," identifying the swing, and entering on retracement

Here's the base method, step by step:

1. **Wait roughly until 10:00 AM.** To be clear — this isn't about staring at the clock and acting at the literal, exact second of 10:00. It's a *round figure* reference — could be ten minutes earlier or later. The point is to let the market settle first.
2. **After that, there are only two outcomes** — either the swing that's built up continues, or weakness shows up and it reverses. The same logic applies whether it's a buying swing or a selling swing: after ~10 AM, either continuation or reversal happens.
3. **Mark the ~10 AM candle** so you can clearly observe from there. From that point, whichever direction the swing is going, you wait for a **retracement**. Once the retracement comes and a fresh support (or resistance) forms, that's where you look to enter — in the direction of the original swing.
4. Importantly, a **single candle poking against the level is not treated as a valid retracement.** It needs to be a real, visible pullback before you treat it as a retracement worth acting on.
5. There's also a simpler, complementary way to look at the same idea via the **closing session** (roughly 12:00–2:00 PM, sometimes momentum shows even a bit later): if the market has moved down, look at where support is holding into that closing window; if it's moved up, look at where resistance is holding. Keep it simple — the fewer variables you track, the easier it gets, especially starting out. So early on: just watch the closing session (12–2 PM) and see what the market is doing there.
6. **Underlying logic**: price can't move in one direction forever — it must retrace at some point. So we wait for the retracement, wait for support/resistance to re-form after it, and enter there.
