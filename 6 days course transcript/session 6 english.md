### Why exit: the three-attempts rule

Risk-management rules repeated through this trade: after the short, the market proceeded AGAINST the direction **three times** toward the same resistance/support level. Each time buyers tried and sellers knocked them down is itself a WEAKNESS sign for the buyers, and the chances of a move against the prevailing flow keep rising. General expectation: when price reverses near your target after repeated attempts — only that much momentum was ever needed (entry ~140; booking anywhere near/below the ~180 region was enough) — the third reversal forming means **exit cost-to-cost**. These few simple rules ARE risk management, discipline, and execution combined.

## The professional entry (recap + new depth)

The whole motive of every daily entry: **the entry should form when momentum starts** — then some momentum arrives, and the SL becomes safe automatically.

- After entry, **5–10 points of favorable move should come almost immediately**; with a small SL, momentum worth around **1:1 should arrive instantly** — same candle or within one-two candles. That's a perfect execution/entry. Today's entry was perfect: proper confirmation, proper timing.
- The earlier the entry, the better — BUT never take an entry by leaving out confirmation. Without confirmation it becomes a gamble ("tukka"), and the probability of the opposing move increases.
- **Entry timing inside the candle:** before the candle fully forms, the entry question doesn't even arise. Entry can form at CLOSING time — when the candle has consumed enough and gives its closing near the higher/lower level. Mid-candle entries are not confirmations; without confirmation the market often just runs further and the entry turns wrong. Take the entry DURING the momentum pull, so the momentum works in your favor.
- **Missed the candle?** Taking a retracement/lower-level entry afterward still keeps you safe: the SL sits below the lows, and if weakness persists the divergence shows up.
- **Never short the absolute low.** If momentum misses and one-two candles form downward, don't short right at the low expecting more fall — that's a bad-probability execution. Take the pullback instead; in options you get a near-support price there.

### Immediate-momentum requirement & self-testing

How do you know you're on the right track? Test yourself: after this session, take **10 entries** and review them. If in MOST entries the stop-loss hits and you find no momentum came after entry — your entries are wrong. Correct behavior: after entry, 5–6 points of momentum arrives (140 → 150, even 155 in the same candle) — more than 1:1 pulled already in the FIRST candle; then any retrace or sideways phase doesn't hurt you. By the 1:2 zone, with a 12-point SL sitting 12 points above, you have a **24-point funnel to play with — safety keeps increasing**.

**Most important point:** a small-target, small-ratio, small-SL system REQUIRES post-entry momentum compulsorily. If it doesn't come, the entry is wrong — improve it. Late entries mean the momentum gets missed; sideways-phase entries hit the SL in maximum cases. Being somewhat EARLY is acceptable — the ratio is better, so even one-two stop-losses stay manageable.

## Confidence — rule #1

Don't create entries in haste. If you feel nervousness in a trade, or sense the confirmations aren't there — the better choice is always to NOT execute that entry, or not trade that session at all. **The first rule: no confidence → no execution.** There's no sense executing without confidence. With confidence there's no nervousness, no emotion. A person isn't ready to trade until they're confident — lack of confidence means the concept or understanding is weak somewhere.

Confidence comes from knowing: your ratios are right, your stop-loss is right, your entry price is right, your overview is right — and knowing your probability. You don't think about ONE trade; you think about **10 trades**: executed the same way, some give 1:3, and 2–4 also hit SL — so nothing that happens today carries lasting impact. That observation builds confidence.

No confidence? Either don't execute, or reduce quantity heavily — one lot, two lots, five lots. The most dangerous combination is **no confidence + heavy quantity** — emotions arrive, fear arrives, everything falls apart. There's an old saying — *"whoever feared is dead"* — and it applies beautifully in markets. If you fear this, this field isn't for you. He acknowledges emotions/fear hit him too when starting; everyone goes through the "let this one case pass and I'll never repeat the mistake" phase — that's start-of-journey stuff.

## Environment — rule #2

The second thing: the environment must be RIGHT to execute. If traveling with family or amid disturbance — no. If traveling alone in a separate room, things closed, KNOWING nobody can disturb you for 3–4 hours until the execution is done — then he prefers executing even while traveling. Otherwise not. At home: kids playing around, family calling — these break execution. The environment must be PEACEFUL — closed room, best if you don't even see anyone coming and going.

**Equipment:** trading one script → one laptop/PC is enough. Mobile is NOT advised for execution (checking prices on mobile is fine; prefer PC for execution). No fancy setups needed — no 6–7 screens unless you're genuinely running multiple things at once (stops running, portfolio running, etc.). One single system is sufficient. Peaceful environment + confidence are the first two requirements; the general execution rules come after those.

So the order: confidence first, environment second, THEN entry/SL rules. The SL-entry setup as taught should come WITH confidence and properly — if the proper entry doesn't come, let the next view/combination form and execute then.

## Stop-loss rules

### Average-size SL → automatically realistic 1:3

It's very important that the stop-loss is **average candle size**. With an average-size SL, the **1:3 target** becomes a general, realistic expectation — not unrealistic; Indian markets make the target realistic automatically.

### The oversized-SL exception

Some cases: the low is quite far away, and putting the SL at the low would make it huge (12–13–15 points) — then the target becomes unrealistic. Solution: DON'T place it at the far low; place it **slightly above the low, slightly bigger than average size**. Simple general rule.

### Management after entry — one rule only

Once the trade is running, management is simple: ALL analysis happens BEFORE the entry. After entry there is NO analysis — even if it feels like the overview is changing, the market is bouncing off resistance, or news arrives — none of that may disturb you. Beginning general rule: once entered, END the analysis right there. What remains after entry: the average-size SL placed near/slightly below the lows (check swing lows — if average fits till the swing lows, great; if points are few, extend slightly).

The single permitted intervention: if the trade approached a REALISTIC 1:3 target — traveled beyond roughly **1:2.2 up to ~1:2.5**, missing the 1:3 by merely **5–7 points** — and then reverses sharply back toward cost, you MAY exit at break-even/cost-to-cost (or touch-negative) rather than ride to a full SL. Otherwise there is no other rule: target hit → book; SL hit → exit.

## Backtesting walkthroughs — Part 1 charts

### The unbiased-day checklist & the standard first-swing rule

A recent past day answered like a checklist: all-time high? yes. Retested? yes. Support made? yes. Bounce after support, then resistance and sell-off? yes ⇒ unbiased/indecision market — both views exist, no bias held. Prior-day instruction: high side = buy (strength), low side = sell (weakness). Difference vs today: THAT day opened into higher levels; today the lows broke.

**Standard rule for bias-less days:** whenever the previous day gave no clarity/bias, take the NEXT day's trade in the direction of the FIRST swing that forms — verified beneficial across that day, the previous day, and the day before.

Two flavors of higher-low entries taught: (1) the usual pace-of-momentum-break entry, and (2) simple continuation on a proper pattern — higher lows forming, resist, dip, turn up — enterable on the reversal candle closing with strength WITHOUT waiting for the high to break; a "proper breakout" is not required.

### Swing reclassification & the two pattern-break types

A buying swing is VOIDED once lows break for **3–4 consecutive candles** — the whole leg is then treated as a selling swing, and buy setups are ignored until a fresh valid pattern break appears. On today's chart: sell-side pattern-break locations existed but weren't traded (buying overview); later, inside the reclassified selling swing, the pattern break printed in one candle, reversed, and moved up = confirmed.

Two points of observation mark a pattern break: (1) **number of candles** — multiple candles push the highs, then a lower high forms and lows begin breaking; (2) **domination** — a single aggressive candle dominates/consumes the previous candle (large body), especially significant when it's the FIRST such occurrence inside the swing (e.g., the 9:45 red candle consuming the ENTIRE previous green when no red had consumed anything before = confirmation of the selling side).

### Against-flow confirmation menu & the two entry methods

Flow-favorable context (all-time high + strong buying momentum): **ONE candle of support is enough** to execute buys. Against flow, confirmations are mandatory — either a **lower high** forms (then execute), OR **double-domination**: buyer dominates a candle → seller dominates → buyer again → seller again; execute ON THE SECOND SELLER DOMINATION. Gap-down handling: higher levels followed by a gap-down → the buying swing created afterwards is still bought per flow (support → pattern break → buy).

So the system's TWO entry methods: (1) flow-favorable — single resistance/support forms in the flow's direction → execute directly on the pattern break without extra thought; (2) against flow — rejection counting: buying candle appears, sellers fail to sustain, seller reverses; support holds, buyers bounce, sellers dominate again = TWO rejections; enter on the THIRD confirmation — or the double-domination trigger above.

**Momentum-miss principle:** against flow without clean candle-wise confirmation, deliberately LET some momentum miss. Wait for proper confirmation and guard against price flipping back and breaking the highs again; letting the lows break down first yields the higher-probability execution.

### Timing law & historical universality

Whatever the FIRST swing of the day (buy or sell), it forms **around 10 o'clock** — support/resistance creation lands in the ~9:45–10:00 window in maximum cases, on any day, verifiable years back. Minimum expectation of a flow-favorable trade: intraday LOW breakdown (for shorts) / HIGH breakout (for longs) — and even many against-flow trades deliver this. Proof offered live: scrolling randomly back to early 2023 (Nifty around 17,000–18,000) and finding the mirrored setups — weekly-high/all-time-high contexts always resolve as either CONTINUATION or REVERSAL, decided purely by observation. Same applies to stocks ("name any stock, I'll show the same chart behavior"). Selling-swing exhaustion heuristic: after a gap-up from highs, the counter selling swing lasts roughly **5, 8, or 10 candles**, then takes support.

### Mirror selling-flow universe

Exact opposite case: market in a selling flow for 3–6 days → sideways close at the lows (weekly/all-time low) → NEXT day opens and creates a BUYING swing ⇒ reversal/retracement case; minimum expectation = **high breakout AND continuation**. Entry nuance: support formed off just TWO candles — identical logic, vice-versa.

### Favorable-buy replay & the best-opportunity doctrine

Template day: continuous prior-day high breakouts → gap up → morning retrace → EXPECT support at 9:45–10 (it printed at the 9:45 candle). Sequence: first red candle, sellers push down again, pullback candle, down again — the THIRD candle's size = confirmation; domination showed exceptional strength at the selling swing's 9:45; post-support the entire wick reversed and highs kept breaking → single-candle entry legitimate, SL below.

Best opportunity in a buying market at all-time highs: a dip giving LOWER LEVELS to buy — regardless of whether the dip comes via a buying or selling swing; identify where the retracement is "enough" (continuous falling has low probability) and buy the support + pattern break. The superior variant: **retracement completed the PREVIOUS day and the fall resumes the next morning** — "there's no better case than this." First-candle neglect: when the dip rejects completely within 1–2 candles right at open, neglect the first candle for structure.

### Classification mini-case

Green candles print, then support starts breaking near 9:45 ⇒ classify the move as a SELLING swing — but do NOT automatically short it: overall flow is buying, no lower high formed, the prior close was at the highs, and after such a big gap-down the drop ALREADY counts as the retracement. Selling skipped.

### Student-guidance block (SL hits, re-entries, exits)

Because the SL is small and entry sits at support, occasionally **1–2 stop-outs happen — accept them; the ratio pays**. Beginners may plan AT MOST two entries, and both only within the SAME swing and only because the support identification was WRONG the first time — never chain entries across different swings (e.g., shorting after the selling swing ended and a buying swing began). Opportunistic cut-and-re-enter is explicitly not advised at the beginning. Re-entry economics: options-buying charges are negligible, so exit-and-re-enter costs nothing material — first SL spent, new support forms, execute again on the fresh pace-of-momentum break. Warning: sometimes after you exit, the support sustains and bounces without you — you must be VERY quick; hence this style is discouraged for beginners ("we prefer peace over executions").

## Swing trading — same concept, changed timeframe

The promised extension: "do nothing — just change the time frame."

- Identify trend/major flow on the **DAILY** chart (a moving average is optional — any trend tool is your choice; nothing else needed) and locate where the supports sit.
- Drop to the **HOURLY** frame to find where a significant/major retracement has completed.
- Execute in the intraday session using the IDENTICAL intraday method (strength buys at lower levels), and HOLD the same entry taken from support for days.
- His personal preference: swings of **3, 4, and 5 days**. BTST, swing, positional — all the same concept; positional trend ID happens on the major (daily) frame. For BTST specifically, an "enough" bounce back suffices; maximum cases exit via trailing.

**Swing entries are selective:** you cannot take swing entries every day — certain setups arrive; WAIT for them. Trade only sessions where the hourly frame shows a significant retracement completed (or buy only after deep falls) — execution probability rises and clarity remains ("only buy"). **Never chase highs:** explicitly not recommended to buy for a swing while price runs at high levels — let a significant retracement come, let support form, then go intraday into that session. Buying-top prohibition detail: after proper retracement, buy when lows aren't broken + a higher low is forming + higher highs stand above it + resistance is breaking.

**Worked swing examples:**
- **21st–22nd December:** pre-fixed buying bias; entered the session on N-pattern/M-pattern confirmation (or when the selling swing completed); HELD from the 21st to near-about the 29th; exit via the TRAILING method — fixed ratios equally valid.
- **21st December 2023 (5-minute walkthrough), two entry options:** Option A (safe side, preferred): support + pattern break → smallest SL. Option B (if A doesn't form): proper-domination confirmation — market makes lows, then 2–3 selling-side candles carry price back near the highs (domination done; note the market had originally fallen FROM the highs), buyers push from the lows upward = confirmation → execute; SL slightly above the intraday low; HOLD and trail.
- **18th–19th June gap sequence:** market fell, gapped DOWN on the 18th, then gapped UP on the 19th and ran — trailing executed on each new INTRADAY low (trailing on major lows equally possible).
- Multi-day trail management narrated: Day 2 — keep trailing; Day 3 — trade going negative → wait; gap-up that isn't sustaining → falling again → keep trailing "till somewhere, then cut."
- Top-down process illustrated with circles on the daily chart: a session followed by significant retracement marks candidate days. Where a retracement ENDS cannot be known in advance — you only observe that a significant one occurred and react to support formation. Big-frame version of the same law: price never falls continuously; big selling swings can't keep forming without bounce backs.
- Hypothetical objection answered: "support is already formed, I'll buy on the 12th" — doing so costs exactly one stop-loss; the correct approach is waiting for the FRESH support identification after the deeper leg down.

**Small-SL swing math:** planning swings with small SLs at 1:3 reward means even 2–3 small-point stop-outs are fine — holding duration is long and targets dwarf the risk. Works best with small quantity and no fear of holding.

## Strike price selection (full revision)

- Default: **ATM** (today's example: the 22,400 strike) or the nearest ITM within **100 points**.
- Beginners: avoid over-analyzing — more checks bring more confusion; the premium differences between strikes build slowly (toward ~20–30%).
- **Divergence check is mandatory** on whichever strike you choose.
- **Volume-spike location:** buying at support → look for the volume spike in the CALL option; selling → in the PUT option; prefer those strikes.
- Preference order: **WEEKLY first, always.** Within weekly: check 100 points ITM first, then 200 points ITM. Monthly fallback: 100 points ITM or ATM. (Anticipated objection answered: "sometimes you take monthly?" — that's HIS flexibility; for students, weekly is always the first preference.)
- Final chooser: pick the strike with the best volume in your favor, the best divergence, the best strength. Three simple pillars: **volume, price behavior, weekly-vs-monthly**.

## Money management, capital growth & expectations

- **Capital increment rule:** increase capital slowly — never 1L → 2L → 4L quickly. Maximum **30% increase at a time**; only after a minimum of **10 trades** (he says 10–20) AND at least **1 month** of successful results; fund the increment preferably from accumulated profit. Main focus = maintaining CONSISTENCY, not doubling capital. Failure mode to avoid: earn 2–3 months, scale up, lose everything — the 3-month process nets zero monetarily. Hard cap: never beyond 30%. Slow and steady wins the race.
- **ROI expectations: zero.** Don't set expectations; follow the process; size positions to your digestion capacity; add capital slowly — ROI builds itself. Claims of sustained 30–40% monthly returns ignore scaling limits ("you'd overtake Warren Buffett"); large capital forces method changes — smaller relative risk, wider SL, smaller targets — ROI falls automatically; big quantities cannot fill at one price, and consistency breaks at very large size.
- **Emotion control:** proper knowledge + proper execution + digestible quantity = no emotion. If quantity exceeds digestion, emotions appear — shrink quantity and scale up gradually; discipline and confidence fix the rest. (Rewatch the SL-placement examples from yesterday/today.)
