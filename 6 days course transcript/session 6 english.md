## Opening & recap

Good evening, everyone. In the last session we completed the base concept and went through multiple examples. Today we continue with more examples and the remaining points (entry rules, management, and mindset).

Starting with an example: if we wait, there's a better possibility of a **buying** setup today, because the market is near its low(er) level. When a weak selling came and price dropped, the overview stays the same — you wait and take the trade per your rules.

## The professional entry

When you take the trade (e.g. a buying-side continuation or a selling-side break), the rule is unchanged:

- **Entry comes on candle close / confirmation**, never before the candle completes. If you enter mid-candle without confirmation, you might lose the "clean" read and the trade gets wrong.
- The whole motive is: **entry should come at a point where momentum starts right after** — so the small SL gets automatically safe.
- If, after the entry, 5-8-10 points of momentum don't come within the first few minutes / candles, then the entry is not that good — price isn't responding.

## The two entry types (recap from session 5)

Two types continue to be used:
1. **Pattern-break entry** — the market makes proper higher-lows/lower-highs (a clean pattern), and you enter on the pattern-break candle after confirmation.
2. **Domination entry** — watching which side dominates (e.g., one-two candle high breaks but domination stays selling; or buyer dominates then seller dominates), and taking the trade on the domination confirmation.

He also stressed: **maximum two entries** on a day — never more than two on the same setup. Only set the trade when the swing completes and a fresh support/level forms.
## The overview + pattern-break flow

When the day's overview is a **selling** read (market came to higher level and reversed, or tested a major level), the first plan is selling; you then look for the **pattern break** on the selling side. If the overview says buying side, you wait for support and take the buying-side continuation.

He reminded that:
- After any move, **a bounce-back always comes** — a falling market bounces off a support; a rising market forms a resistance, then either reverses or continues. This is basic market nature.
- If you're not sure which day to buy, then, when there's **no prior-day clarity, trade in the direction of the next day's first swing.**

## The crucial rules of execution & environment

He laid down the execution rules in order:

1. **Confidence is rule #1.** If you don't feel confidence on a trade, **don't execute** — even if it means not trading that session. If you do want to trade, reduce the quantity (one/two/five lots). Fear is the worst state; *"whoever is scared is already dead."* Confidence comes from knowing your SL/primes/overview are right — you think in terms of 10 trades, not 1.
2. **The environment must be peaceful.** Execution should be done on a desktop/laptop, not a single small mobile screen, with no distractions and not juggling many things at once. "Peace over execution."

**Analysis stops at entry.** Everything — the analysis, the confidence-building, the understanding — happens **before** the entry. Once you are in the trade, there is **no more analysis**; you simply follow the pre-planned SL and exit. You don't keep re-analyzing a running trade.

## Stop-loss & trade management rules

- **SL placement:** if the nearest swing low is very far, don't place the SL exactly at the low — place it a bit above the low, roughly a touch bigger than the **average candle size**. Simple general rule.
- **Once running:** the SL is plotted; management is simple — you exit on the break-even (or at SL); targets get booked when they hit. One single rule: **exit at break-even / manage the stop; nothing else after entry.**

## A personal loss story — why the environment matters

He shared a real story to make the point:
- While travelling with family (a trip that included a halt at Shirdi on the way to a work-trip to Bombay), he had a **running trade** that he simply could not manage — timing for the trade crossed, and he couldn't cut it mid-way.
- When he got back, a **big amount** (well into the lakhs; he doesn't recall exact) — roughly a **single-day erosion on a ~15-lakh option-buying position** — had been cleared.
- The lesson: **don't compromise on your execution environment.** If you're travelling, or can't watch the charts, have someone else manage the trade, and keep peace. He personally always wants someone else watching the critical level when he's away. Never take execution lightly — anything can happen.

## Money management (simple)

He reiterated the simple rules — nothing fancy:
- **Risk management:** already covered — a ~1:3 reward ratio is favourable; keep the risk small.
- **Trade management:** analyse **before** the trade; don't re-analyse after; follow the SL and plan.
- **Quantity sizing:** don't take a very big quantity with a single small-SL pricing — keep small quantity so the small-SL method holds. If you try to deploy huge capital, the entry method has to change, the SL becomes bigger, and the target/reward shrinks. So "50 things" can be discussed, but the **simple rules are always the best.**

## Swing trading (weekly frame bonus)

He touched on swing trading:
- On the **weekly** time frame, observe **where the market is relative to support**; on the **daily** frame, mark simple swings. You can plot a moving average if you like, but you don't strictly need indicators — the pattern/flow works.
- For **index options**: observe the **major flow** (buying side for many days = trend). Then when the intraday retracement comes **near the support** while the flow is buying, you take that same intraday entry and **hold**.
- Same method applies to BTS/T and positional trading. He said he prefers **peace over aggressive execution**, and this holding style fits longer/simpler setups rather than fast scalping on a small screen.

## Trader vs. investor & expectations

- **Don't chase big expectations.** Start with what you can digest; grow the process gradually; don't fix a big target and then chase it. If you keep expectations, you'll plan around them and get distorted. Just follow the process.
- **Trader or investor — pick one.** He personally is purely a trader and doesn't do long-term holdings/investing. The market gives (roughly) ~15% ROI long-term; exceptional returns like Rakesh Jhunjhunwala's are not extractable by everyone. Mutual funds, real estate, diversification — he noted briefly that most people can't consistently beat index funds over a long (15-yr) horizon; those who claim huge compound returns via Instagram reels are rare/misleading.
- **Capital limitation:** with a very big capital you can't keep the same small-SL high-win-rate method — you have to scale up the SL and shrink the target, which changes the whole approach.
