## Opening & recap

Good evening, everyone. In the last session we discussed the N/M patterns and the "two-legs" setup, and the market's domination. Today we build further on the **execution** side — specifically how to take a **small stop-loss with a bigger target**, and what challenges come with it.

## The challenges with quick, momentum-based execution

To trade like this, a few things must be true:

1. **Your execution has to be quick.** Timing matters. If you are slow, the risk management won't hold.
2. **Don't enter before the momentum** actually starts. If you enter too early (in the "parked"), the market may not move — the stop-loss gets hit. Entering early is not a *safe* entry.
3. **The professional entry is "getting on a moving train and getting off a moving train."** You enter while the market moves in the momentum — and exit while it's still moving. If you sit in a "parked car" (sideways), nothing happens and you are better off staying out.

So the ideal entry is one where the market supplies the momentum right after you enter. The stop is small, so **the momentum has to come quickly** to keep the trade safe.

## Average candle size & realistic targets

Because the stop is small, the target must line up with the session. The basis is the **average candle size** — the average range/height of the session's candles:

- Measure the average candle size (sum highs-lows / number of candles), or estimate by the general candle size and take a slightly larger-than-average reference.
- Since the SL is placed very near the low, the base criterion for expected momentum is at least one average candle / a few points of move. Because the SL is small, an entry at a good spot sees the market jump 5-10 points quickly — that jump makes the SL automatically safe.
- **Don't set the SL too big** — then the target ratio becomes big and the entry gets sloppy, and in many cases the target is missed. Keep the SL small and the target realistic.

### The key entry rule
**If after your entry a 5-6 point momentum doesn't come in your favour almost immediately, your entry needs improvement.** Small SL + small target = the entry must be precise. Without this, you can't run a small-SL, small-target system.

## The two types of entries

There are two entry types:
1. **Type 1 (easy to identify):** the market makes a high, and then the selling domination forms **below** the high — creating a lower-high / lower-low pattern. Once that low breaks with domination, you act.
2. **Type 2:** the high breaks one by one (only one-two candles against), but the domination is on the selling side — a slower, weaker-pace case where you need the confirmation from a clean pattern-break candle.

In swing cases previously discussed, when "weakness" is forming but the market keeps making one-candle highs, ultimately the domination resolves one way.

## Timing is crucial

Most of the setup happens around **~10:00 AM**. The timing of when you look to identify the first swing's support/resistance matters a lot — the **10 AM candle** is the reference. By ~10 AM the market has usually settled and starts building its first support/resistance.

He walked through real days (e.g., March 1, Feb 29, Feb 23, Feb 25) showing: the market rarely just keeps running one way through the whole morning — it retraces near 10 AM, forms support/resistance, and that's when you mark things. If the market opened and made huge swings every single day, everyone would short every day — the market "resists" that. So it's usually uncertain/build-up for the first bit, then a decisive flow.

So: observe the flow early, find the ~10 AM reference, and wait for the **momentum break / flow break** — where price, having been in a direction, breaks the pattern and the momentum follows.

## Confirmation via rejection & "third rejection"

- When the market approaches and **rejects** a level repeatedly (signals of selling/weakness), the confirmation builds after proper domination and rejection.
- He referenced "**third rejection**" — if no third rejection happens, the entry forms on the *clean pattern-break candle* after domination. The 3rd rejection is a nice-to-have; the clean break is the confirmation.
- If the overview is selling and the market tested a major low (e.g., March 1), that's **against** the selling direction and in the trader's favour — so a direct "rejection" call isn't forced; you still follow the break.

**Meta-rule:** once you're in a trade, **follow your plan** — don't change it mid-trade. If you've decided "no action before 1:2 / exit after 1:3," do exactly that, even if price runs past and then reverses.

## Volume & option-strike confirmation

For a selling/continuation trade in options, **volume** confirms the move:

- When the market falls, a **significant volume increase** in the option is a confirmation of a confident move (momentum on the strike).
- If the market falls but **volume does not increase**, it's a **divergence** — not a confident/strong move. Don't chase it.

How to read volume:
- For general direction you may watch the **futures chart**, but if you're executing in **options**, watch the **option strike's own volume**. In Nifty specifically, we look at the volume of the option strikes (the call/put premium is what matters more for us).
- Compare the call vs put premium moves — whichever shifts defines the direction. Then check whether the **overview** also matches; if so, proceed in that direction; otherwise give the highest weightage to **price**.

## Order types & exits

- **Entry:** prefer a **limit order** (get filled at a better price below/at the level); you can also use a market order when trading a small quantity. Use **SL / SL-M** orders for protection.
- **Exit:** he prefers **not** to pre-plot the target in most cases — he exits manually on a **market order** at the target (feels better / lets the trade run), though a pre-plotted target is fine too.
