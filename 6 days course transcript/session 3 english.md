## The market has limited possibilities

First, let's look at what possibilities the market even has. We generally see the market open, go down sometimes, go up sometimes, come back down after going up, sometimes make higher lows and sometimes lower highs. So here's the key: **price has a limited set of possibilities.**

### Why limited? The two-activity framework
"दो ही लोग है — one is buyer another one is seller — and दो ही तरह की activities हैं, इसके अलावा तीसरा activity नहीं है — buyer can buy into the market, seller can sell into the market" — There are only two people: buyer and seller. And only two types of activities: buying and selling. Nothing else happens in the market.

Because activity is time-limited (Indian market has a specific trading window), the **kinds of cases** that can get created are also limited. If the market traded much longer, more of the same-type swings might form — but the **base nature stays the same**.

That base nature: after any swing is created, price either **proceeds toward the swing's bottom level**, or it **proceeds to a higher level** — i.e., it goes down to a lower level, or it retraces, holds higher, and goes further up.

### Limited possibilities extend beyond the first swing
"यहां से अब क्या-क्या possibility है — and वो भी limited है... जैसे ये दो possibility limited है — तो इसके आगे भी जो जो possibility है — वो define करेंगे तो वो भी limited है" — Just like the first swing has limited possibilities, the second swing and all future swings also have limited possibilities derived from the same base patterns.

Why is it uncertain? Because if the market simply ran in a straight line, everybody would make money — that's not the market's behavior. It stays **uncertain, unexpected, and unpredictable** — all three. Unless the world economy and the market's timing change drastically, no huge exceptional change is going to happen from here.

### The confidence this gives
So you should never be sitting at your charts feeling "what is happening? where is the market going?" That happens when your **probabilities aren't pre-decided**. If everything is pre-decided — if every scenario and probability is analyzed in advance ("after any swing the market can go this way or that") — then when the market starts doing exactly one of those possibilities, you gain **confidence in execution**. You know: "this is happening now, so from this point price can go this far." And nothing else can happen besides the limited set. That's how confidence comes in execution.

## The two execution legs

"concept is like हम दो executions plan कर रहे हैं — एक execution होता है हमारा market के major overview के base पर, and दूसरा जो creation होता है वो होता है market के morning behavior पर" — The concept is we are planning two executions:

1. **Leg 1 — Major overview-based execution:** Based on where the market sits in the major range (higher level / lower level / all-time high / support area). This is the broader context trade.
2. **Leg 2 — Morning-session/swing-based execution:** Based on the strength/weakness confirmation of the first morning swing. This is the specific entry trade.

Both legs operate simultaneously. The overview tells you the directional bias; the morning swing tells you the specific entry point.

## Two-point overview execution framework

"अभी आपको सिर्फ अगर मैं दो point दे दूँ — इस system के लिए तो वो दो यही है" — For this system, two points only:

1. **If market is at major lows/support and falling → short on resistance.** Overview: market is at support area, falling continuously → on resistance formation, short it.
2. **If market is at major highs and falling → buy on support.** Vice versa: higher level testing, falling → on support formation, buy on overview.

### Trend-following overview
- **Buying trend** (higher highs, higher lows) → buy on support per overview.
- **Selling trend** (lower lows, lower highs) → short on resistance per overview.

"market अगर buying trend में चलता है — तो अगर retrace करेगा — तो support बनने पर — overview के according buying करेंगे" — If market is in buying trend and retraces, buy on support per overview.

## The two/three types of swings

In the last session we discussed the first swing — it makes 5-6 candles on one side. But two types of buying swings exist; the pace of momentum need not always be high. A buying swing can be sharp, or it can be slower. And it doesn't matter what the pace of momentum is — the swing marking is done on the basis of the swing itself.

### Two pace-of-momentum types
Concretely, a buying swing either:
1. **Type A (Strong):** Creates a **resistance** and **sustains it** — market creates resistance at one level and holds there. "buying swing बनाया है — and buying swing में market एक ही resistance को create कर लेगा — that resistance sustain हो जाता है" — Strong swings have continuous momentum without retracement.
2. **Type B (Retracement-type):** Keeps breaking highs one candle at a time, then reverses — "buying swing में ही इस तरह का buying swing बनेगा — कि एक-एक candle करके highs break कर रही है — और फिर वहाँ से resistance लेकर reverse हो रहा है" — The market doesn't sustain; it keeps going and then reverses.

**Identify the pace of momentum first.** If a swing is built in a very slow/weak way after ~10 AM, be a little careful with your entry — a bit more cautious execution. If the pace of momentum is much higher / more aggressive, there will be different methods and different expectations for the intraday target.

Also, don't rush to call something on the basis of a single red candle. Example: the market made a buying swing, retraced (a red candle), then came back up — so the pace of momentum of that buying swing is different from what a single red candle would suggest. If you misjudge it in a hurry, you'd be wrong.

### Reading the flow
"ultimate flow देखना है — green red green red तो बनेगा — आपको flow देखना है — क्या नीचे की तरफ जा रहा है" — Don't get confused by alternating green/red candles; look at the overall directional flow to identify which swing is being formed. Green-red-green-red will always form — that's just candles. The question is: what's the overall direction?

### Single-candle rule (detailed)
A market move can be created from one, two, or three candles — but what you must observe is whether, after opening, the market **first went down or first went up**. So:
- If the market keeps making those (four, five, six) candles in one direction and **sustains with momentum**, that direction is the swing.
- **If first candle is red and sustains** (next candle doesn't consume it) → it's a selling swing confirmed.
- **If first candle is red but next green candle fully consumes/reverses it** → neglect that candle, count the swing from the reversal point. "अगर पहली candle जो भी बनाया है — उसको अगली ही candle पूरा consume कर जाती है — reverse कर जाती है — तो जो उसके बाद का overall swing creation है — उसको count करेंगे"
- A **single candle poking against the level is not treated as a valid swing** unless it's followed by confirmation (a real retracement).
- If a small doji or small candle forms after the first candle, it's not a confirmed swing yet.

## Strength vs. weakness — where to act

As we discussed, when making an entry the very first thing that should hit your mind based on the overview is the expectation:

- If the market is moving **up** on any day → the overview expectation is that it will form a **resistance** and retrace. But that's not guaranteed — it can instead be a **continuation** case.
- If the market is in a **selling trend** and moves up on a given day → on **resistance formation, short it**.
- If the market is in a **selling trend** and is falling → on **support formation, buy it** (it won't keep falling straight down without a bounce).

So the general tendency:
- **Falling market** → first thing in your mind should be **buying** (it will take support somewhere and go up).
- **Rising market** → first thing should be **resistance then retrace** (it will form resistance and go down).

But you are not forced — the better you observe the **major overview** (where in the major range the market is, and how it's responding), the better position you can execute according to the overview.

### Core logic: retracement during creation = weakness
"किसी भी buying swing में — अगर जो buying swing जो बन रहा है — creation हो रहा है — अगर creation में — market क्या कर रहा है — ज्यादा retrace कर रहा है" — If during swing creation the market retraces more, it means less strength. "अगर retrace नहीं करता है — लोग पहले ही खरीदते चले जा रहा है — उपर के price पर — तो logic समझे आप — कि retrace क्यों नहीं करता है — क्योंकि strength है — it's a sign of strength" — If buyers keep buying at higher prices without retracing, that's a sign of strength.

### Big swing = strength indicator
"बड़े buying swing का क्या logic है — कि इतना strength है — कि usual swing creation से थोड़ा buying swing creation हुआ — और उसमें भी market retrace नहीं कर पाया — बड़ा swing बना है — मतलब क्या हुआ है — कि दूर तक buyers ने dominate किया" — Big swing = buyers/sellers dominated far and wide. The SIZE of the swing itself is a strength indicator. Small swing with retracement = weakness.

### Two-point weakness identification
"इसमें 2 point के base पर सिर्फ observe किया जा रहा है" — Only two points are being observed for weakness:
1. **Retracement/recovery during the swing** — "recovery कि selling swing आ रहा है — उसमें क्या recovery आ रही है बार बार" — Recovery coming again and again in the selling swing = weak.
2. **Swing size is small** — "selling swing बड़ा नहीं" — The selling swing is not big = weak.

Both points together give you a stronger probability judgment.

## The expectation (weak vs. strong)

When you identify whether the first swing has strength or weakness, set your **expectation** accordingly:

- **Weak selling swing** → a small support forms → **intraday high breakout** is the general expectation (recovery).
- **Weak buying swing** → **intraday low** — a reversal expectation.
- **Continuation** case → the market keeps going; if strong, target accordingly and you'll get it.
- In a **reversal pattern**, the minimum expectation is an intraday high / intraday low breakdown.

He stressed he wasn't asserting the "facts" strictly, but that the **minimum expectation** you keep should be these. He also said: as a trader, execute where the *probability is highest* — on the weak selling swing (support → intraday high), because that's what market nature most often does.

### Probability target: 60–70%
"minimum से 60-70% — कि आप सिर्फ पहला swing देख के बता दें — और इसका probability जो correct होगा — वो 60-70% होना चाहिए" — The target is 60–70% probability from swing observation alone. "हर एक दिन सही नहीं होगा — 100% यह नहीं है" — Not every day will be correct. It's not 100%. But 1-2 sessions will be wrong because the market is exceptional and unpredictable. That's acceptable.

## Four second-swing patterns after a buying swing

The instructor walked through four possible second-swing scenarios after a first buying swing, with interactive Q&A from students:

1. **Retraces to support = weakness → short.** Market retraces all the way back to the support level. This means the buying swing was weak. Short it.
2. **Retraces but makes higher low, then continues = strength → buy.** Market retraces but doesn't reach support — makes a higher low instead, then continues up. This is the continuation pattern.
3. **Makes higher low then lower high and falls = reversal.** Market makes a higher low (seems strong), then makes a lower high and falls — this is a reversal pattern.
4. **Sustains at higher level without retracing, continues.** Market doesn't retrace at all — stays at higher level and continues. This is a strong continuation.

### Categorization: 1 continuation, 2 reversals
"जिसमें एक continuation pattern है — और दो देखेंगे तो reversal pattern है" — After the first swing creation, in buying swing there is **1 continuation pattern** (higher low → continuation) and **2 reversal patterns** (retrace to support → reversal; lower high → reversal).

### Specific reversal patterns
- **V-shape recovery → W-formation:** Market makes V-shape recovery, then W-formation to go up (continuation).
- **Fake break-up pattern:** "market कई बार उपर जाके — फिर retracement करके — फिर दुबारा fake break-up देता है — और फिर वही से एक sharp retracement देने ता है नीचे की तरफ" — Market goes up, retraces, gives a fake break-up (appears to break resistance), then sharp retracement downward. This is a common reversal trap.

## Exhaustion concept

"वो फिर exhaustion भी देखा जाता है — कि अगर इतना उपर चला गया है — तो profit booking होगा — तो continuously एक line में भी उपर नहीं जा सकता है" — Even if market has been going up continuously, it can't go up in a straight line forever. Profit booking will happen. If exhaustion enters the market, it will retrace.

"यह ऐसा नहीं है कि strength का closing है — उपर continuous चला गया है — मतलब कहें कि नहीं अब तो continuation ही होगा — ऐसा होता है क्या? नहीं होता है" — Just because market went up continuously doesn't mean continuation is guaranteed. Exhaustion will cause retracement even in strong trends.

## Swing observation vs. closing overview — DON'T CONFUSE

"ये swing observation है — इसको मत भूलेगा — इसलिए लोग mess up कर देते है" — This is swing observation — don't forget it, that's why people mess up.

"अभी तो उपर गया — ये positive ही overview है — अगर उपर गया है — आप major overview मुझसे पूछेंगे — ये positive है — अगर day देखेंगे — day positive है — लेकिन बात क्या हो रही है — वो positive तो गया है — लेकिन क्या उसमें strength है continuation होने का — या weakness है" — The day may close positive (overview positive), but the swing may show weakness. These are **two different concepts**.

"ये point ध्यान में रखें — कि वो दोनों concept different है — ये हम जो कर रहा है — हम strength weakness का observation कर रहा है — वो overview है" — Keep this in mind: swing observation (strength/weakness) and closing overview (positive/negative close) are different things.

## Risk management / small SL

If you keep a small stop-loss you get a better **return on investment**. If your ratio is fixed but you try to **consistently capture very big momentum every single day**, your failure rate will be high.

"जादा बड़ा अगर momentum expectation रखेंगे — तो जादा cases में नहीं आएंगे — ये market nature है... जितना बड़ा momentum capture करना जाएंगे — तो आपका failure rate ज्यादा होगा" — Big momentum expectations → higher failure rate. But "छोटा momentum तो बन रहा है ना हर जगह — बड़े-बड़े swings नहीं बन रहा है — बट छोटे-छोटे तो मिल रहा है — तो उसका probability क्या होता — automatically increase हो जाता है" — Small momentum is available everywhere; probability automatically increases when you target smaller moves. Keep the SL small and the expectation realistic — trade the highest-probability move.

## Two execution approaches: complicated vs easy

Instructor's personal approach: "इसलिए I am following more complicated stuff — क्योंकि उसमें छोटा entry है — entry हो गया right time पर target जल्दी आ जाता है — बाहर हो गया" — Small SL, quick entry at right time, target comes fast, exit. But "उसमें complications है — उसमें emotion strong चाहिए — discipline ज्यादा चाहिए — mindset strong चाहिए" — Requires strong emotion control, discipline, mindset.

The easy system: "जो easy system है — उसमें बड़ा SL है — तो momentum भी फिर थोड़ा ज्यादा चाहिए" — Bigger SL, needs more momentum, but simpler to execute. Choose based on your current level.

## Bonus: option selling / the script

The instructor noted that option selling is more comfortable/preferred for this method — "जो एक easy execution backup system बताये गया — जिसको selling में जो और ज्यादा बहतरीन काम करेगा" — Because you give more time for confirmation and execute on proper confirmation, unlike buying which requires quick momentum capture.

**Option selling on every expiry day:** Student asked if selling should only be done on expiry days. Instructor confirmed every day is essentially expiry day now (weekly expiries), and selling can be done daily.

He also answered a question about switching scripts: he trades Nifty (and banknifty). Everyone's script has slightly different behaviour — so rather than hopping between option scripts and expiry dates each week, pick a script, practice it, and build a "steady hand" — like a racing driver who practices on his own car. Changing scripts too often dilutes the feel. (Professionalness point.)
