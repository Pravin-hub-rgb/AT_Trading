# Concept: Overview Building

This file is the condensed concept version of the overview theory derived from the six sessions.

---

## Core question

What is the market likely to do next, given the current structure?

Not:
- “What will happen exactly?”

But:
- “Which side has the higher probability?”
- “Where is the next support or resistance likely to form?”
- “Is the market buying or selling at the current level?”

---

## Market nature

The market has limited possibilities.
It is not free to move anywhere without structure.

Typical behavior:
- opens, moves up or down
- sometimes retraces
- sometimes holds near the top or bottom
- often forms support or resistance before continuation or reversal

This is why you study structure instead of reacting to every candle.

---

## Step 1: Find the recent range

Look for recent swing high and recent swing low.

Ask:
- Where is the recent high?
- Where is the recent low?
- Is the market trading near the top, middle, or bottom of that range?

This tells you whether the market is in a buying or selling zone.

---

## Step 2: Read the market relative to the range

### Near recent highs
Likely scenario:
- resistance is around
- sellers may be active
- the market may fail to push higher

But do not blindly short just because it is high.
Wait for rejection or supply behavior.

### Near recent lows
Likely scenario:
- support is around
- buyers may be active
- the market may bounce

But do not blindly buy just because it is low.
Wait for demand behavior.

### Mid-range
Likely scenario:
- uncertain or waiting market
- do not force direction
- let a clean swing form

---

## Step 3: Decide the overview direction

### Correct interpretation of near highs / near lows
This is a critical point:

- Near a recent swing high = likely **resistance zone**. Do not treat it as automatic buy just because price is “high.” The default is to look for rejection or failed upside.
- Near a recent swing low = likely **support zone**. Do not treat it as automatic sell just because price is “low.” The default is to look for support hold or buying demand.
- The overview is not: “buy at the top because it cannot fall straight down.”
- The overview is: “price near the top means watch for resistance; price near the bottom means watch for support; only act when the level confirms.”

### Buying overview
- market is holding above support
- buyers are defending lower prices
- the market is not breaking down badly
- a pullback toward support is a buying area

### Selling overview
- market is failing at highs
- sellers are defending resistance
- the market is not breaking higher strongly
- a bounce toward resistance is a selling area

### Neutral overview
- no directional commitment
- no strong support/resistance behavior
- wait for confirmation

---

## Step 4: Define the action plan

### If bullish structure
- plan for buying at support
- do not chase at the top
- prefer pullbacks into demand

### If bearish structure
- plan for selling at resistance
- do not force trade at the bottom
- prefer bounces into supply

### If uncertain
- stay out
- wait for the next clean swing

---

## Step 5: Check swing strength/weakness

A swing can be strong or weak.

### Strong swing
- keeps moving in one direction
- little retracement
- clear momentum
- continuation probability higher

### Weak swing
- loses momentum quickly
- retraces more often
- support/resistance breaks and rejections happen
- reversal probability higher

This helps you decide if the move is likely to continue or reverse.

---

## Step 6: Wait for confirmation

This is the key discipline.

Never act only on:
- a single candle
- a random feel
- one red or one green candle

Wait for:
- support holding
- resistance rejecting
- a clean break or rejection pattern
- momentum after entry

---

## Step 7: Trade the highest probability, not the strongest emotion

Your job is not to trade the most exciting move.
Your job is to trade the most probable move.

The overview helps because it tells you:
- where the market is likely to react
- which side to respect
- where to place the trade plan

---

## Quick formula

Overview formula:

Recent swing high/low + current price position + support/resistance + strength/weakness + confirmation = trading plan

---

## Most important practical correction

A common misunderstanding:
- “Near the top = buy because price can't drop straight down.”

The better logic is:
- near highs, the market often faces resistance first
- buying only happens if the structure remains bullish and a proper support/confirmation continues

Likewise:
- near lows, the market often faces support first
- selling only happens if the structure remains bearish and resistance rejection confirms

So the overview is not “buy because the top is high” or “sell because the bottom is low.”
The overview is “where is the market likely to react, and on which side is probability higher?”

---

## Final rule

Before entry, always ask:
- Where is the recent swing high/low?
- Where is price relative to them?
- Is this a buying or selling environment?
- Where is support/resistance?
- Is the level holding or rejecting?
- Is there confirmation?

If the answer is unclear, do not trade.

That is overview theory in its simplest form.
