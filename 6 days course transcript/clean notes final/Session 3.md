## The market has limited possibilities

The market opens, falls sometimes, rises sometimes, comes back down, makes higher lows sometimes and lower highs sometimes. Key: **price has a limited set of possibilities.**

### Why limited? The two-activity framework

Only two participants exist — buyer and seller — and only two activities exist: buying and selling. No third activity exists anywhere in the market.

Because activity is time-limited (the Indian market has a fixed trading window), the KINDS of cases that can form are also limited. Longer sessions would only create more same-type swings — the base nature never changes:

**After any swing is created, price either proceeds toward the swing's bottom level, or holds higher and proceeds further up** — down to a lower level, or retraces-and-continues up.

### Possibilities stay limited beyond the first swing

Just as the first swing has limited outcomes, the second swing — and every future swing — has only limited possibilities derived from the same base patterns.

Why does uncertainty exist at all? If price ran in straight lines, everybody would profit — that isn't market behavior. It stays uncertain, unexpected, and unpredictable — all three. Unless world economy/timing change drastically, nothing exceptional breaks this.

### The confidence this gives

Never sit at charts wondering "what's happening, where is it going?" — that feeling exists when your probabilities aren't pre-decided. Pre-analyze every scenario ("after any swing, price can go here or there"); then when the market starts doing exactly one of those possibilities, execution confidence appears: "this is happening now, so from here price can travel this far." Nothing outside the limited set can occur.

## The two execution legs

1. **Leg 1 — Major overview-based execution:** from where the market sits inside the major range (higher level / lower level / all-time high / support area). The context trade.
2. **Leg 2 — Morning-session/swing-based execution:** from the strength/weakness confirmation of the first morning swing. The entry trade.

Both run simultaneously: overview supplies directional bias; morning swing supplies the specific entry point.

## Two-point overview framework

For this system, just two points are enough:

1. Market at major lows/support and falling → **short on resistance formation**.
2. Market at major highs and falling → **buy on support formation**.

Trend-following version:
- Buying trend (HH, HL) → retrace → support forms → buy per overview.
- Selling trend (LL, LH) → pullback → resistance forms → short per overview.

## The two/three types of swings

The first swing runs ~5–6 candles on one side. But TWO buying-swing types exist — pace of momentum needn't always be high:

1. **Type A (Strong):** creates ONE resistance and SUSTAINS it. Continuous momentum without meaningful retracement.
2. **Type B (Retracement-type):** keeps breaking highs one candle at a time, takes resistance there, then reverses. Doesn't sustain; extends, then turns.

**Identify pace of momentum FIRST.** A slowly/weakly built post-10AM swing demands extra caution on entry; an aggressive high-pace swing brings different methods and different intraday-target expectations.

Don't rush judgments off a single red candle: buying swing → red retracement candle → back up means that swing's pace differs from what one candle implied. Hurried misjudgment = error.

### Reading the flow

Green-red-green-red always forms — those are just candles. Watch the ULTIMATE FLOW: which overall direction is developing? That identifies the forming swing.

### Single-candle rule (detailed)

A move may be created from one, two, or three candles — what matters is whether the market FIRST went down or FIRST went up after open:

- Keeps making 4–6 candles one way AND sustains with momentum → that's the swing.
- First candle red and sustains (not consumed) → selling swing confirmed.
- First candle red but next green FULLY consumes/reverses it → neglect that candle; count whatever overall swing forms AFTER it.
- A single candle poking against a level is NOT a valid swing unless followed by real-retracement confirmation.
- A doji/small candle right after the first candle = swing not yet confirmed.

## Strength vs weakness — where to act

Entry thinking starts from overview expectation:

- Market moving UP any day → expect resistance + retrace — but continuation instead is possible.
- Selling trend moving UP that day → **short on resistance formation**.
- Selling trend FALLING → **buy on support formation** (no straight-down without bounce).

General tendency: falling market → think BUYING first (support somewhere, then up). Rising market → think RESISTANCE-then-retrace first.

You're not forced — the better you read the major overview, the more precisely you execute with it.

### Core logic: retracement during creation = weakness

If a swing retraces MORE while being created → LESS strength. If buyers keep buying at ever-higher prices without retracing → strength; it doesn't retrace BECAUSE it's strong. It's a sign of strength.

### Big swing = strength indicator

A big swing means domination extended far beyond usual — even within it, no retrace was possible. SIZE itself is a strength indicator. Small swing with retracement = weakness.

### Two-point weakness identification

Weakness is observed on exactly two points:
1. **Repeated recovery/retracement inside the swing** — recovery coming again and again = weak.
2. **Small swing size** — not extending = weak.

Both points together sharpen the probability call.

## Setting the expectation (weak vs strong)

Once the first swing reads strong or weak, set the minimum expectation:

- Weak selling swing → small support → general expectation: **intraday high breakout** (recovery).
- Weak buying swing → reversal toward the **intraday low**.
- Continuation case → ride accordingly if strong.
- Reversal pattern → minimum expectation = intraday high / low breakdown.

Not asserted as absolute facts — these are the MINIMUM expectations to carry. Execute where probability is highest: e.g., the weak-selling-swing play (support → intraday high) — that's what market nature most often delivers.

### Probability target: 60–70%

From first-swing observation alone, correct-call probability should be ~60–70%. Not 100% — 1–2 sessions will be wrong because the market stays exceptional and unpredictable. Acceptable.

## Four second-swing patterns after a buying swing

1. **Retraces fully to support = weakness → short.**
2. **Retraces, makes a HIGHER LOW, continues = strength → buy** (the continuation pattern).
3. **Higher low, then LOWER HIGH, then falls = reversal.**
4. **No retrace at all — sustains high and continues = strong continuation.**

Categorization: **1 continuation pattern** (#2) and **2 reversal patterns** (#1 and #3).

Specific reversal structures:
- V-shape recovery → W-formation → continuation upward.
- **Fake break-up:** rally → retracement → FAKE break above resistance → sharp retracement downward. Common trap.

## Exhaustion

Even a continuously rising market cannot go up in one straight line forever — profit booking enters, exhaustion enters, and it WILL retrace. A strong close plus continuous upside does NOT guarantee continuation; exhaustion causes retracement even inside strong trends.

---

## Swing observation vs closing overview — DON'T CONFUSE

This is swing observation — forgetting it is why people mess up.

Market went up ⇒ overview positive ⇒ day positive. But the real question: did that move contain STRENGTH for continuation, or WEAKNESS? A positive close and a weak swing coexist — **two different concepts**: strength/weakness observation via swings ≠ positive/negative close overview.

---

## Choppy days warning

For now, don't study choppy days — markets making 2–3–4 swings in mixed directions. Study days moving in ONE clear flow (one clean selling flow or one clean buying flow).

---

## Risk management — small SL & realistic capture

Small SL → better return on investment. Fixed ratio + trying to capture HUGE momentum every single day → high failure rate. Bigger momentum expectation = arrives in fewer cases (market nature). Small momentum forms EVERYWHERE — big swings are rare, small ones constant — so its probability automatically increases. Keep SL small, expectation realistic, trade the highest-probability move.
