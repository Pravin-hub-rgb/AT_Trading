## Price above everything

- The decision should ultimately be made on **price**, above all else — "Price is God." What we trade is price, so what we analyze is price. Price action is the action of price.
- Big institutions and fund houses don't stare at loaded-up charts. Charts are secondary for them; they watch **raw price numbers** — green means up, red means down. That is what everyone is ultimately watching.
- Other confirmations exist (volume, option strength/weakness, etc.), but when they go AGAINST what price shows, execution still follows **price** — because price carries the highest weightage of all.
- Nuance: going against price via other confirmations is rare and scenario-based — exceptions exist, but in general price rules.

## Why no indicators

- Execution here is purely price-based. Any concept should be **universal** — applicable to any script, any future, Nifty, any stock. A concept that only works in one spot isn't a concept.
- Volume/order-flow tools are at most secondary confirmations. On some days other indicators show clear weakness yet execution still happens on price — the weightage of every factor matters: price first.

## The nature of price = the nature of the market

- Retail traders, institutions — everyone operates on the same price movement. The nature of price is the ultimate, final thing.
- **The market never moves in one direction.** This single fact is the foundation of everything — it's why maximum people lose money, and also why some make money.
- Nobody has ever seen a market go straight up forever. The general nature: an uptrend meets resistance → weakness → correction → fresh buyers enter → market proceeds higher again. Same mirrored for downtrends: buying appears → small correct → continue down.
- The market doesn't go from point A to B in one move: it typically makes **two or three swings** before its final high, and multiple swings down before its final low. So during a trade, a retracement does NOT invalidate your swing — it may simply be building the next leg.

## Only two kinds of trading days

1. **Continuation** — the market keeps going in the direction it started.
2. **Reversal** — the market flips direction instead.

Nothing else gets created outside these two.

### Identifying reversal vs continuation — how strong is the bounce?

After a fall and a bounce back:

- **Weak bounce** (small buying candles, no follow-through) → **retracement**; selling will likely continue. Selling opportunity: enter on the next candle after the weak bounce, stop above the bounce high.
- **Strong bounce** (big buying candles, momentum) → **reversal**; do NOT sell. Wait for the reversal to complete, then look for buys.

Same logic after a rally: weak pullback = continuation (buy); strong pullback = reversal (don't buy).

## The core method

1. **Wait roughly until ~10:00 AM** — a round-figure reference (ten minutes either way is fine). Let the market settle first.
2. After that, only two outcomes exist: continuation or reversal of the built-up swing — same for buying and selling swings.
3. **Mark the ~10 AM candle**, then wait for a **retracement**. When retracement completes and a fresh support/resistance forms, enter there in the direction of the original swing.
4. A **single candle poking against the level is NOT a valid retracement** — it needs a real, visible multi-candle pullback. Single poke = noise; multiple candles = signal.
5. **Closing-session read (~12:00–2:00 PM):** if the market fell and holds selling pressure into 12–2, it's a negative close → next day can test lower levels. If it rose and holds buying into 12–2, positive close → next day can test higher levels. Strong momentum can extend a bit past 2 PM.
6. Underlying logic: price can't move in one direction forever — it must retrace; wait for the retrace, wait for S/R to re-form, enter there.

### Option strike selection
Use **ATM or nearest ITM** strikes. Avoid far OTM — cheap-looking but poor liquidity and wide quickly. Prefer **weekly expiry** for liquidity/tighter spreads.

### Entry timing — nothing before 9:45 AM
The first minutes after open are pure noise — the market hasn't found direction, especially on gap openings. **No trades before 9:45 — hard rule.**

### Position sizing
Start with **2–3 lots maximum**; scale only as account and consistency grow, proportionally — never impulsively.

## Four types of opening cases

1. Opens and **falls directly** down.
2. Opens, goes **sideways** a while, then falls.
3. Opens flat and goes **straight up** (mirror of case 1).
4. Opens flat, rises first, then **corrects downward** — the reversal-day setup.

## Probability & overview

The more consistently your overall read ("overview") of the day is correct, the higher the probability your entries and targets play out — execution flows downstream of getting the overview right. Once understanding is solid, execution isn't the hard part.

## Exit strategy

When in profit, stay in until the swing **completes or reverses** — don't exit prematurely — but always keep a stop in place. Examples: 29th and 21st entries were held until the swing showed completion/reversal signs.

## Stop-loss placement method

- Buying trades: SL **below the last swing low**.
- Selling trades: SL **above the last swing high**.
- Swing structure determines distance: more candles = wider stop; tight swing = closer stop. Mechanical — measure from actual structure, don't guess.

## Option selling — success rate vs R:R tradeoff

- Selling only at **confirmed** support/resistance ≈ **80% success rate**; selling at any random candle drops to **50–60%**.
- Tradeoff: the high success rate needs wider stops → more risk per trade. So position sizing + stop discipline decide profitability — hence starting with 2–3 lots.

## Perspective & mindset essentials

- The main aim of the course: change/improve the **perspective (nazariya)** with which you view the market — not to chase something that magically says "support forming here, resistance there."
- Two people can look at the exact same chart and draw opposite conclusions — the difference is entirely perspective.
- His own shift began when he stopped chasing predictions and started observing the **market's nature** instead. The end goal (direction) stays the same; the method of reading changes.

## Theory Q&A

- **Confused whether a day's close is positive or negative?** Check **second-half momentum**: closing toward the higher end of the range in the second half = positive close (good buying-side momentum), and vice versa. Also: if a strong buying swing has clearly formed, the session can still count as positive overall even if things turn slightly negative afterward.
- **Low-of-day break after 10 AM:** a post-10AM selling swing may temporarily break the low of day — expected. But once that selling swing completes and bounces, breaking down again *after* 10 AM is generally avoided / treated differently.
