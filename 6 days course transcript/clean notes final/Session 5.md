# Session 5 — Entry Precision, Average Candle Size & Small-SL Momentum (Tuesday, March 5, 2024)

---

## Recap & why "N/M" is just a label

Last session covered the N/M patterns, the two-legs setup, and domination. Today builds the EXECUTION side: taking a **small stop-loss with a bigger target**, and its challenges.

Saying "buyer domination till highs, reverse to lows, back to highs" every time is clunky — so N/M shorthand exists. It may sound like beginner stuff, but it's kept deliberately simple. It's a **universal concept on every timeframe** — big swings, small swings, even within one candle. Large-frame observations are reused at small scale for confirmation.

## Every concept combines into a single execution

Consistency cannot come from one isolated concept. Everything learned so far — base concept, swings, overview, domination — activates together **inside each execution**. Examples may look familiar from videos, but without the full concept chain they can't be replicated consistently.

---

## The challenges of quick, momentum-based execution

1. **Execution must be quick** — slow timing breaks risk management.
2. **Don't enter before momentum starts** — entering early ("parked") means no movement and a hit SL. Early ≠ safe.
3. **Professional entry = boarding and leaving a moving train.** Enter while moving, exit while moving; parked cars go nowhere.

Ideal entry: market supplies momentum immediately after you enter. Small stop ⇒ **momentum must come quickly**.

---

## Average candle size & realistic targets

Small stop → target must match the session via **average candle size**:

- Measure average range (sum of highs-lows ÷ candles) or estimate slightly above general candle size.
- SL sits near the low, so minimum expected move ≈ one average candle / few points. A good spot sees 5–10 points instantly — that jump makes the SL automatically safe.
- Don't oversize the SL — it inflates targets, loosens entries, misses targets. Keep SL small, target realistic.

### The ~5-point immediate-momentum rule (most important checkpoint)
Plan SL by average size; after entry, roughly **5 points of favorable momentum should arrive almost immediately** in maximum cases. That instant push protects the small SL. No quick push = entry needs improvement.

### Key entry rule
Small SL + small target demands PRECISION — without immediate follow-through this system cannot run.

## Two execution scenarios: flow-favor vs flow-against

### Scenario A — WITH the flow
Buying trend, market retraces: **no N/M check needed**. Support identifies via pace-of-momentum break → execute directly.

### Scenario B — AGAINST the flow
Selling INTO buying flow (retracement expected from highs): needs **confirmation entry** — minimum TWO rejections, executable on the third. Buying-flow swings don't reverse continuously; domination must be proven first.

### Morning preference
He prefers morning-hour entries personally — best momentum lives there. Overview trades work later too (won't fall continuously; support → bounce), but the visible morning opportunity is itself high-probability: weakness building → minimum expectation = intraday-low breakdown → execute.

### Counter-scenario handling
Market runs to HIGHS with weakness instead → plan the RETRACEMENT trade there — same two-rejection + third-confirmation logic at the high. With-flow direction → wait for support + confirmation → execute.

## Timing rules

- **Nothing before 9:45** — skip any setup appearing earlier; standard rule.
- **Avoid after ~11:12** for morning-session trades; absolutely nothing after 12:00.
- **10 AM = the formation window:** even picking charts blind, the first swing's S/R forms around 10 o'clock — demonstrated by marking 10-AM candles across many dated charts (some just after, some exactly at, occasionally earlier). Keep at the back of your mind: the level isn't formed yet; it will form within a couple of candles, maybe after breaking lows first — but it WILL form there. Continuation-vs-reversal afterward is secondary observation. Strength → price near highs; weakness → near lows → confirmation.

### Why the market behaves this way
Post-open, price runs 5–8–10 candles one way, resists, then plans direction. Five-minute candles × 5–6–8–10 ≈ the 10-o'clock round figure (post-9:45). We ADAPT to the market's rhythm — we don't drag it to ours. If every day opened with huge one-way runs everyone would short dips/buy rips — impossible; uncertainty first, decisive flow second, S/R forming inside that window. Market opens at 9:15 for years — universal observation.

**Generic day anatomy:** open → up → down → retrace-back close; small TF: open → high → low → pullback close; later-half momentum takes support and pushes higher. Gap-down variant: buying-flow market gaps down, builds support, climbs into higher swing — hence "won't keep falling."

Working WITH direction-favor AND timing-favor is why identification succeeds most of the time; fighting either creates messes. Timing + break together raise probability of where/what happens.

## The first swing is never traded inside

His maximum entries show: he does NOT trade inside the first swing's continuation. Let it complete (rarely over 4–5–6 candles; max-to-max 5–6 from yesterday's tests; absolute bound 8–10), THEN take continuation OR reversal. Entering mid-first-swing is how people get chopped.

Does the first swing ever continue hugely? Class confirmed in chorus: generally NO — 1–2 exceptional days only (even March 1's runner resisted en route). Certainty that resistance WILL come is your edge.

## Pace of momentum break — the identification engine

### Definition
Observe the NATURE of the first swing (selling/buying): activity pattern, speed. Wherever the established pattern turns UNUSUAL — speed or structure — that's the **pace-of-momentum break**: highest-probability S/R location.

Classification nuance: first green candle fully consumed + low broken → NEGLECT it; classify as selling swing. Note the swing's characteristic recovery pattern (e.g., single-candle recoveries); that nature persists across swings — exceptional deviation IS the break = market creating S/R.

### THREE break types
1. **Candle-wise:** established rhythm breaks (alternating 1-green-1-red becomes 3 consecutive selling candles).
2. **Domination-wise:** aggressive candle consumes/reverses sharply. (Aggressive moves count under domination.)
3. **Candle-size/aggressive:** candle far above session average prints — unusual size itself signals the break.

### Rhythm reading predicts the next candle
Rhythm "2–3 greens, 1 red rejection, greens resume" ⇒ next red will likely be absorbed, highs broken again. NO action on lone reds — pattern intact. Break arrives only via: 2–3 sideways candles / lows breaking after a high, OR one big domination red (size vs previous candles). Until then wait — and the break WILL come ("break will definitely come"): market nature, maximum-time behavior of any session's first swing. We simply wait for that moment.

### Confirmation needs follow-through
One big candle alone isn't confirmed: big size at crucial timing but consumed nothing, no next-candle continuation → wait more. Confirmed when: big selling reversing off support + domination candle CLOSES + first-time upward continuation follows. Big candle + close + follow-through = confirmed break.

### First break = highest-probability spot
Wherever the FIRST break prints is where S/R forms. Live example: market fell, green candle printed, FOR THE FIRST TIME next candle's seller dominated but FAILED to break lows — buyer reversed it again. Pattern broken; confirmation within two candles; third candle took support and broke highs → apply high-low-break concept → continuation confirmed → "train has started." Flow-favor → direct buy on the pace break — the 4th March buying plan exactly.

### Market nature is the only certainty — hence universal
Nothing in markets is certain EXCEPT nature: price cannot travel one direction forever — major overview, smallest timeframe, intraday first swing alike. Exceptional days exist but aren't nature. Trading THAT nature everywhere makes the concept universal. Higher price extends, higher the probability it resists — resistance itself is certain even if what follows isn't. That certainty is the edge.

### Point-to-point identical identification
Follow the rules and EVERYONE marks the same point — nobody earlier or elsewhere. "It came here means it came here." Zero ambiguity.

## The two entry types (+ two weakness build-ups)

1. **Type 1 (easy to identify):** high made; selling domination forms BELOW the high → lower-high/lower-low pattern; low breaks with domination → act.
2. **Type 2:** highs break one-by-one (only 1–2 candles against) while domination stays selling-side — slower pace, needs clean pattern-break-candle confirmation. (The demonstrated counter-flow short was Type 2 — a buying-swing case.)

Two types of weakness/selling build-up: (a) sell → fall → clean continuation; (b) weakness building BUT each candle's high keeps breaking — up, sold, new high, sold again — domination without LH/LL structure; analyzed via option chart, volume, price strength.

## Confirmation via rejection & the third rejection

Repeated rejection at a level builds confirmation after proper domination:

- Minimum TWO rejections; entry possible ON THE THIRD. Sequence: buyer bounce → sell from high (rejection 1) → dip builds weakness → buyer pushes → sold again (rejection 2) → buyer pushes AGAIN but can't reach prior highs = extra weakness sign → falling on the third attempt = "the train has started."
- Third rejection confirms: market can't even reach the prior high before rolling over; odds of opposing IT collapse. That's the trigger.
- No third rejection? Entry then forms on the clean pattern-break/domination candle — arriving momentum is price's self-confirmation.
- Flow exception: overview selling + market testing a MAJOR LOW (March 1 type) = test AGAINST the selling direction = in your favor → direct call allowed WITHOUT three-rejection wait. Selling AT higher levels after a major-high test → full rejection check applies.

**Meta-rule:** once in the trade, FOLLOW YOUR PLAN — decided "no action before 1:2 / exit after 1:3"? Do exactly that even if price overshoots then reverses.

## Volume & option-strike confirmation

### Session volume signature
Open spike (first 1–2 candles carry volume — market nature) → mid-session reduction → the BREAK of that reduction marks where domination occurs (maximum cases) → close spike. Option charts differ slightly (positions execute there); futures or option charts both observable.

- Falling market + significant/double volume increase in the option = confident-move confirmation (proper domination shows double volume).
- Falling WITHOUT volume rise = divergence / not confident → take extra confirmation before executing.
- HUGE volume printing ON the support-identification candle = confirmation in itself.

### Divergence reading
Spot breaks highs, option doesn't follow; or option sags to lows / sustains lows early while spot holds — extra weakness/strength info. Often appears BEFORE spot reverses → "execute even more confidently." Reverse case: spot breaks lows but option stops breaking, buying starts early = buying-side confirmation.

### Which chart for volume?
Watch OPTIONS for volume when executing options (not futures). Stock traders watch futures volume as the norm. In Nifty he watches the OPTION STRIKE's own volume. Futures stay usable for general direction — personal choice (futures even better for analysis).

### Strike selection via volume break
Scan ATM + ITM (~1–2–3 strikes): usually ONE shows the volume break/divergence, others don't. Strikes normally run nearly identical — differences matter. **The strike with the volume break is strongest; prefer it** (it carries distinct comparative divergence).

### Don't over-deepen
Option-strike behavior offers further weakness identifications (sustain-or-not) as price rises — but you don't need that depth yet; the taught points suffice.

## Risk management — reversal rules

- **Momentum beyond 1:2 then reversing → exit near cost:** target being 1:3, if >1:2 traveled and reversal comes — don't panic-exit, don't ride to SL; cut cost-to-cost when price nears cost. That IS the risk-management rule.
- **Cost-to-cost exit unlocks a second trade** with fresh direction planning.

### Advanced techniques — NOT advised yet
- **Early exit on weak reversal (before 1:2):** requires strong confirmations + perfection through practice. Premature exits followed by the intended move create pressure/regret and disrupt mindset — avoid initially.
- **Support-break cut-and-re-enter:** experienced only — entry on support, bounce comes, bounce RESISTS and a successful sufficient reversal looks imminent from a good entry → cut right there; it will break support, form NEW support, take it again. Skill-based; not for beginners.

- **Entries:** use LIMIT creatively, almost like market orders — buy-limit placed ABOVE price acts as buy-stop (crosses level → buys); bargain limit below fills only lower down. Orders used: LIMIT, SL, SL-M. Caveat: true limits don't fill if price never retraces there. Small quantity → plain MARKET order fine. Protection via SL/SL-M.
- **Exits:** plotting targets is fine; he prefers NOT plotting — manual market-order exits let trades breathe.

## Retracement-expectation logic (the short case)

Market built support then ran continuously from lows toward highs — won't continue forever. Around 10 o'clock the pattern break comes → market RESISTS → resisting, it RETRACES. Minimum expectation: support break + some pullback. Plan both outcomes from entry: reversal against you → cost-to-cost exit; otherwise plan the second trade from there.

### Big swings shrink reversal probability
Strength-swing case: continuously big swing holding higher lows; retracement came but swing size grew very large — whenever one-directional momentum gets that big, probability of full reversal back to lows (selling domination) automatically drops. Size tempers expectations; pattern-break observation still locates the turn.

## Revision checklist (before any entry)

1. **Timing** — 9:45 start; ~10 AM formation window; ~11:12 cutoff; nothing past 12.
2. **The swing** — which type; NEVER inside the first swing.
3. **Two entry types** — below-high domination vs one-by-one highs with selling domination.
4. **Major overview** — builds the plan; morning view = direction, major overview = 3–4-day context.
5. **Pace-of-momentum break** — three break types.

Additional rules:
- Confirmatory entries + selling suit beginners best — safer, consistent; M-pattern execution suffices for safe-side entries; for selling, the two-swing system is best too.
- Choppy days barely matter with a morning-session plan — morning momentum + swing-sized SL/target suffice in maximum cases. Support-SL hit? Plan the next trade.
- Maximum **two entries per day** in the beginning.
