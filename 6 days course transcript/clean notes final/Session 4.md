# Session 4 — Confirmation, Domination & the N/W Setup (Monday, March 4, 2024)

---

## Operating idea & recap

We don't trade every candle. We take **more confirmation** — wait for support/resistance to form, execute on either side (or via option selling). No FOMO about missing momentum; execute calmly after proper waiting. Confirmation comes automatically from proper waiting + a proper rejection/reversal. The hard part is only calling resistance WHILE it forms — that needs deeper concepts.

## The two-layer system: confirmatory → identification

- **Layer 1 (confirmatory):** if a buying swing reverses, there's a 70–80% chance price reaches the support area → sell the call (or buy a put) THERE.
- **Layer 2 (identification):** judge weakness/strength just by WATCHING the swing itself — prepare early for whether reversal or continuation is more likely.

Execution still happens ONLY on confirmation; preparation starts earlier. Never mix systems — other concepts are backup plans; the primary path is confirmation-based execution. No rush: especially when selling, because we play **both legs**, not one.

**Clubbing:** combine what the overview says with what confirmation shows — both together form the trade plan. Checking the overview alongside the N/W/M read isn't strictly mandatory, but combining saves wrong trades (e.g., an N turning into an M), reduces trade count, and pushes probability higher.

## Reading weakness/strength inside a swing — retracement carries the weightage

Two things observed in any swing: its **size** and how deep the **retracement** against it runs. **Retracement always carries the higher weightage**: deeper retrace = weak swing = reversal generally expected. This is the base of the weakness/strength observation.

### Execution condition for reversal-based entries
A reversal-based buy executes only when the move has reversed **70–80%** AND strength suggests the market will actually test the intraday high — not merely because price reversed. Rejections or option weakness can save you from bad entries.

For even higher probability, tighten the trigger to **80% instead of 70%** (demonstrated on a ~138-point swing retraced ≈80%).

## Overview before reaction

The major overview dictates action even when the first move goes against you:

- Buying overview + dip forming a selling swing → wait for support to hold → buy.
- Selling overview → don't flip to buying on a short-lived buying swing. **The overview protects you.**

Sequence always: build overview → plan direction → react to the intraday swing inside that plan.

### Simple overview rules
- At high levels / all-time high → won't fall continuously.
- At lower levels → won't rise continuously.

### Overview protects from bad shorts
At an all-time high we don't short — even if a buying swing reverses. Nuances: weekly highs count like ATHs; and it's not an absolute ban — if the MORNING observation itself builds a selling case (buying swing at ATH followed by V-reversal), short per the morning view; the major overview then governs the next structural entry (higher lows + high breakout → buying side).

### Case study: ATH + sideways expiry
Market near ATH, sideways expiry close, then falls → buying overview: won't fall continuously; will make support and bounce back — even if full reversal takes multiple sessions and dips repeat. From ATH with retracement running, a good bounce off support is generally expected.

### The "10%" exception (inverted case)
Open drops immediately and confirms buyer strength through V-reversal → reversal-based buying leg executes; matching the major overview means NO second position — single leg, ride momentum, exit.

## The "two-entries" idea — when to plan both

Both signals (morning view + setup) combine toward buying → plan two entries on dips. Only one aligns → ONE entry; after its SL, no forced second entry.

### Three sub-rules for the second entry
1. **First entry hits target with sufficient momentum → close the day** ("let the day close green" — more early green = healthier psychology). Exception: leg 1 got NO momentum and was never booked, and leg 2 then forms per overview → executing leg 2 IS allowed. (Leg 2 is positive-expectancy statistically, but adds mental pressure — hence the beginner default of skipping.)
2. **First stops out + second setup forms per overview → execute the second entry.**
3. **No lower high formed → invalidation:** if price keeps breaking previous highs instead, the selling-side overview is dead that day — skip regardless of P&L potential.

### Priority when views mismatch
Morning view and major overview conflict → **prioritize the morning view**; execute it first, plan the next entry only after it exits.

Second-trade ROI vs mental pressure: taking it raises overall ROI but also pressure — beginners who already hit target #1 skip it.

## Domination & repetition ("double domination")

The market keeps revealing which side dominates:

- Selling swing that CAN'T sustain, buyers lifting at higher levels → after retracement, market may rise again.
- Sellers repeatedly dominating (double domination) → selling-side continuation more likely.

### Reversal = strength confirmation
Reversal means strength confirmed: sellers pushed, couldn't sustain, buyers pushed back to higher levels.

### Trapped traders fuel the move
Buyers trapped at higher levels panic-exit on reversal, creating fresh selling that fuels the move. Cross-checking the OPTION chart supports such trades.

### Double-opposition probability escalation
Any side's domination confirmatorily opposed TWICE → the opposing side's probability becomes very high. A market rarely fully reverses 70% and keeps flip-flopping beyond that.

### Max 2–3 bluffs
Reversal bluffs happen at most 2–3 times — which is why two entries are planned (covering continuation or reversal).

Domination cannot continue indefinitely — that's simply not the market's nature.

### Never switch sides in confusion
Confused bias → do NOT flip buy/sell/buy. Act only after MOMENTUM arrives and congestion breaks, proving one side dominates. Switching in confusion ruins accounts.

## N, M & W patterns — full mechanics

Read the day's structure via N/M/W patterns marked with colored boxes (long rectangles = N; squares = M/W):

- **W:** selling domination reversed TWICE (by buyers) → bullish. Highest probability.
- **M:** buying swing reversed TWICE (by sellers) → bearish. Highest probability.
- **N:** single reversal — selling swing → buyers fully dominate → buyers fail → sellers continue (or mirror image). More frequent than M, still good probability.
- **Reverse-N:** mirrored structure.

Anti-misconception: people draw W as "higher low printed → execute" — NO. Observe whether the swing's DOMINATION actually reversed. Related: successful domination = market travels low back up to highs, proving the original seller weakened somewhere.

Frequency: N forms more often; M is rarer but hits far fewer stop-losses — double-domination reversal is inherently rare.

### Proper W requires previous-high testing
A true W must TEST the previous high. Since swings count at 70–80%, the remaining distance needs little momentum — compare against the PRIOR high to judge real strength.

### Formations must complete before ~11 AM
These patterns form in morning hours only — not random up-down chop later. Take W/M trades in the morning, NOT afternoon. One demonstrated exception: second-half execution acceptable only while price still held near previous highs — had the SL zone broken first, no trade at all that day.

### N forms near 10 AM naturally
An N needs repeated dominations — impossible instantly. Three swings can't exist by 9:30; ~9:50 possible; in ~9 of 10 cases the N completes near 10 AM anyway.

### N-to-M transition: N's SL will hit
When an M forms after an N, N's LAST LEG is precisely what reverses under the new domination → N's stop-loss hits essentially every time. Expected behavior, not a flaw (~90%).

### Two entries in highly-confirmed cases
Full W (selling-swing failure → buying swing → buying failure → buyers dominate) = highly confirmed → plan two entries: first on the N (expect SL), then on the W (expect target). But only ONE trade runs at a time — take the W after the first exits.

This is the general rule everywhere: never run two same-direction positions simultaneously; re-enter only after a proper exit (including stop-out) and a fresh overview-aligned structure. Both legs share the same SL marking/line.

### Conservative step-down option
Emotions/confusion high or SLs hitting repeatedly → step DOWN one level: trade only W/M/V-shape reversals. Fewer executions, each very high win-probability.

### Setup frequency balance
Don't pick a setup so rare it appears only 2–3 times monthly — enough candidate sessions must exist to cherry-pick the best cases.

## Single-candle rules

Three distinct rules — don't mix them:

1. **First-candle neglect:** FIRST candle fully consumed/reversed by the next → neglect entirely; count the swing from the reversal point.
2. **Single candle from intraday low:** huge single-candle bounce straight off the intraday low usually does NOT continue directly upward — expect resistance; don't rush buys into it.
3. **Single candle AFTER settling = valid:** after 2–3 candles the market has settled; a single-candle reversal IS valid then. But in the two-leg system, straight low-to-high blasts have reduced probability — keep concepts separate.

### Wick-wait rule
Bounce off support making wicks → WAIT; watch whether lows get revisited. Execute only on the proper confirmatory retest. Related: single-candle drop continuing directly at higher levels (gap-up continuation, no real selling activity) → direct continuation unlikely; expect rejection → wait rejection → support → buy. This candle rule alone saved multiple first legs across backtests.

## Timing rules — 9:45 detailed

Before 9:45 the market isn't settled; execution inside the opening 3–4 candles isn't preferable. Trades before 9:45 are very rare for him.

Unsettled mornings = high volatility = fast either-side momentum = general tendency of SLs getting hit. Executing from the 9:45 candle onward avoids most of this — following this timing rule alone saved multiple legs across the shown backtests.

## Stop-loss placement & risk management

- **Never park the SL exactly at the extreme:** slightly beyond/inside (bit ABOVE intraday lows for longs). If price travels that extra bit, the idea is already dead. For shorts: near the intraday high / previous major swing high.
- **Consistent rupee-loss per trade:** SL AMOUNT should be similar across trades; points differ per condition — QUANTITY is your control variable to normalize money at risk.
- **Range too big → reduce quantity,** and book earlier since momentum arrives relatively smaller.
- **Against-overview trades: exit early** — the overview says counter-moves aren't continuous; exit on first decent adverse bounce/momentum.
- **NO TRAILING:** never trail SLs to entry. Either fixed ratios (exit when ratio arrives) or hold. Trailing has negative points; he personally never trails.
- **Big SL but rarely hit (+ decay):** bigger SL ⇒ lower hit-chance; while selling, price hovering near entry lets theta work for you. That's the crux.

## Exit strategy

- Playing two legs? First leg exits at **1:1.5–1:2** — sufficient.
- Momentum arrived → book directly.
- Sideways holding: position decays (selling side) or waits; exit when sufficient momentum finally shows.

## The losing/ROI side & why selling

Confirmatory entries have HIGH hit rate but longer duration and bigger stops → overall ROI lower. Fine for beginners.

Drawbacks acknowledged: smaller target ratio, bigger SL, lower R:R — but steady green P&L builds beginner mindset.

**8-of-11 stat:** in his marked backtest 8 of 11 sessions closed positive (within the sample: 1 day × 2 SLs, 2 days × 1 SL each). Consistency builds psychology.

**Realistic returns:** monthly expectations of 50–80% are unrealistic here; think 20–30%.

**Option-buying capital limitation:** large capital can't deploy efficiently — e.g., 60,000 quantity at a 200–300 premium can't fill in a small range without price jumps → worse fills → lower overall probability. Small capital earns great percentage returns. Capital principle: small capital suits buying; big capital naturally migrates toward SELLING over time (investments + monthly selling).

**Selling duration-match advantage:** bigger SL = longer hold = more theta captured, PLUS the direction edge from confirmations — double advantage for sellers.

## Psychology & discipline — the root cause

Psychology problems occur when you lack confidence in YOUR execution setup; discipline loosens when execution lacks clarity. Clearer understanding → automatic discipline.

Even traders with 8–10 years still fear intraday and lose — exactly why this easy, green-generating setup exists first: mindset before returns.

Missing entries disturb psychology: N/M entries don't need split-second decisions, but his current system does; strong mindset comes from practice — otherwise 2–3 good sessions precede sudden loss streaks.

3-day SL streaks are digestible in high-R:R systems: one winning day covers three losing days.

Soft-hearted/emotionally sensitive traders should avoid very small SLs — use the safe-side setups instead.

## The "one step ahead" idea & the ultimate game

Key to small stop-losses: IDENTIFY BEFORE ENTRY — read what the market is doing before final confirmation.

Ultimate game: identify the coming final domination BEFORE it confirms, enter right there → tiny SL, good entry. Flow: buying swing → weakness spotted inside it → reversal expected → the N/M is FORMING → enter before the last domination confirms → tiny SL, 1:2–1:3 momentum follows.

Daily overview corollary: once a level is built, the market does NOT come back to retest it in maximum cases — that's why well-placed stops survive.

### Level-up tradeoff
Each level up adds complication but raises ROI: favorable R:R, smaller losses, targets approaching 1:3. The two-leg system eventually outperforms even W/M.

## Backtesting principles

- **Always backtest worst-case scenarios**, not best-case.
- **Homework sequence:** test the setup thoroughly — order: W/M → N → then observe the two-leg identification system.
- **Marking system:** green box = positive close; red box = negative; long rectangle = N; square = M/W; small box = SL outcome, big box = target outcome. Neutral/50-50 days either color (personal choice). Ignore SMALL boxes on his charts — those belong to the previous system's testing marks; count only this system's long rectangles.
- **Mindset-building path:** begin with this system even from a negative mindset; continue without haste 1–3 months; confidence builds automatically, then advance.
