## Recap of the base method

The method works off how the market settles and the swing forming around that settlement — based on the **previous day's close**, identifying the first swing in roughly the **~9:45–12 window**, entering on simple support/resistance formation. This gives entries with better clarity and a more confident read of the day. Today extends this into the bigger picture — the **major overview**.

## What the "major overview" actually is

Observing, at a large level, what is happening in the market:

- **Chart preference:** daily timeframe works; his personal choice is **5-minute charts zoomed out** — the same data appears on daily candles but 5-minute keeps more detail.
- **Data requirement:** at least **6–10 trading sessions visible** on the zoomed-out chart.
- The core question: **where is the market trading within its major levels** — running at a high level, or performing at a lower level / inside a range?

### Two-outcome framework at lower/upper levels

When the market arrives at lower levels (or upper levels), exactly two things can happen:
1. **Bounce back** from support (or rejection from resistance)
2. **Continue lower** with lower highs (or continue higher with higher lows)

The market can't stay in the middle — it must pick a direction.

### When marking gets confusing — the mountain pattern

If the market makes one big swing and reverses fully within a single session (up then all the way back down), that forms a mountain — big divergence between buying and selling sides. Consider the **mid-point** of that mountain as a candidate mark; intermediate points can be marked too if continuation occurs from either side. Watch the big swings created at the market level, mark around them, and conclude from price's relationship to those ranges.

### Marking two ranges

Comparison is normally based on **two ranges**:
1. The **recent range** — the current/nearest range, generally the smaller box, marked from recent swing highs/lows.
2. The **next range** — support/resistance marked from the next, bigger, older swings.

Example: recent Feb sessions (around the 21st, 24th, 25th–26th, 29th, up to Friday 1 March) crowd into the recent range while the larger swings define the next S/R — this tells you which level the market is actually performing from on a given day. All range/S-R marking is done on the basis of buying/selling swings.

### Range updating on breakouts

While price stays inside the smaller range, the overview is based on that range. Once it hits an all-time high or breaks any level, the **new range replaces the smaller range** and the overview shifts. Mark → observe within → level breaks → update → re-observe.

## Pace of momentum — the core mechanism

Pace of momentum = **speed of momentum**: at what pace is the market moving? How many candles does it take to cover a distance?

- **High pace:** fast movement — fewer candles, continuous movement without retracement; sharp with stronger domination.
- **Low pace:** slow — more candles, more retracements along the way; the swing isn't sharp.

A single buying swing may look identical in direction but differ completely in velocity. Whatever the pace, **swing marking is done on the swing itself** (start and end). When two references are marked (major support and major resistance), plot as many cases against them as possible — the market responds to respected levels depending on its condition and how the swing was created.

**Fibonacci connection:** fib is plotted from the START of the momentum swing to where the momentum COMPLETES — and pace determines where completion lands (high pace = faster/fewer candles; low pace = slower/more).

**Reversals/continuations trigger on pace-of-momentum BREAK,** not merely highs/lows breaking — direction changes when the pace changes versus the prior swing.

Swings exist at every scale — intraday, weekly, zoomed-out big swings — same rules; the concept is universal across timeframes.

## What moves the market: supply & demand

- Market rising with higher lows, bought each time at higher prices ⇒ **demand higher** than supply.
- If buyers were strong enough, price wouldn't fall TO a support — so when price does fall to a level, **supply dominated there**: nobody was buying; sellers kept pressing until buyers showed up.
- **100-quantity example:** with 100 quantities available and more buyers than sellers, someone pays more — shortage lifts price, hence higher lows at each level. Price falling to support means supply exceeded demand at those higher prices.
- Indian markets are regulated, so extreme behavior is rarer, but the principle is identical: **price moves on supply-demand domination.**
- General behavior: price doesn't jump from a major low straight to a major high.

## Using the general tendency

We can't force the market — but its nature can be USED:

- **At all-time high / higher level:** if it falls, it won't fall continuously — support forms first, then bounce. Retracement from highs happens in steps.
- **At lower level / support:** if it rises, it won't rise continuously — resistance forms, then pullback. Recovery from lows happens in steps.
- **Continuous reversal is NOT expected** from either extreme. Swings are the market's nature.

Most people lose money precisely because they FIGHT this swinging nature. Using it instead changes the whole game. This is not a strategy — it is **market understanding**, a perspective. Take positions IN LINE with expected momentum (buy on support, sell at resistance), never against it.

## Execution method & risk sizing

Beginner/professionalism stage: plan a **small entry → small stop-loss**. Big targets with a small stop are fine IF quantity stays small:

- Trade goes against you → loss stays small despite adverse momentum.
- Trade goes your way → profit smaller too — acceptable; the beginner's key is LOW MENTAL PRESSURE.

Once pressure rises, discipline breaks and emotion takes over — "the job is over." Saying: **whoever is scared is already dead.** Feeling fear/emotion mid-trade is itself a signal — exit; whether target or SL was coming doesn't matter; the mental state isn't right for execution. Fix system and emotion first, then execute — small quantity.

### Big SL + small quantity (NOT small SL)
Use a LARGER stop with SMALLER quantity — don't place tiny stops. Bigger SL + small quantity keeps pressure manageable.

### Acceptable R:R range
If 1:3 or 1:4 isn't available, exit at **1:2 or even 1:1.5** — peace of mind over max R:R. Green panels matter more than squeezing the last points.

### Positive mindset from high success rate
Prefer methods with HIGH SUCCESS RATE — seeing green panels repeatedly builds the confidence trading requires, even at lower R:R.

## The backup (easiest) execution method

A deliberately simple setup: no premature small-SL/small-target complications. Because you allow MORE confirmation time and execute only on PROPER CONFIRMATION, execution becomes straightforward and safe: wait → confirmed support → execute. The catch elsewhere: small-SL styles demand PRECISION — imprecise entry means no follow-through momentum and you're out.

For this style, **option selling is the preferred, easier route** — more confirmation time, comfortable holding (he'd rarely seen losses on this base method). Buying on top is fine but adds risk. In sideways markets selling positions keep profiting — decay works for you.

## Right mindset of execution

Know each and every step in advance — not worrying about where the market will go. From the overview you already know it won't fall continuously: sit peacefully, observe; when proper support confirmation prints, buy small. Calm and methodical, never rushed.

## The backup plan overall & advancing threshold

This course carries a BACKUP PLAN: anyone not yet ready for the complicated stuff can run this simple method profitably and keep doing it while leveling up. Get the proper process working successfully and continuously first, then deepen.

**Confidence threshold before advancing:** confidence is essential — once it arrives (consistent losses stop appearing, things start changing), THEN switch methods. Don't rush to complicated systems before basics are solid.

## 9:45 timing rule and morning candle observation

**No execution before 9:45** — the market hasn't settled: direction unfound, volatility high.

**Opening candle pattern:** any day opens with either **2–4 green candles** or **2–4 red candles** — nothing else exists. Just watch which forms; that's the day's first data point.

## Option execution — buying vs selling

Capital-size rule:
- **Small capital** → option BUYING (he personally wouldn't sell options on small capital).
- **Big capital** (where buying stops scaling well) → option SELLING — safer here because the base method is confirmation-based, positions hold longer, bigger stops fit.

Either way the overview decides the side. Walkthrough mechanics (selling read / market at highs):
- Intraday high made and NOT held → rejection candle → **sell the call**.
- Falls, support forms → buy on confirmation at the lower level; aggressive buying starting → exit the sold lot.
- Believed-in buying swing FAILS → small-quantity PUT with stop near the swing.
- Pure sideways → the call/put position decays in your favor; exit on confirmation once momentum shows.

## Strength/weakness determination — the decision framework

### After a selling swing:
1. Wait for recovery.
2. Does recovery reach the intraday high?
   - **Yes (V-shape reaches intraday high)** = STRENGTH — buyers exist. Do NOT sell calls there.
   - **No** = WEAKNESS — market can't push back up → sell calls.

### After a buying swing:
1. Wait for retracement.
2. Does it reach support?
   - **Yes (reaches support)** = weakness — buying swing failed → sell.
   - **No (forms higher low instead)** = strength — buying swing held → buy.

### Complete sequence
Market falls → support → strength recovery reaching the intraday high = sign of strength. Don't wait for full breakout — **execute at 70–80% recovery**; sell the call right there rather than waiting for the last 20%.

## Two decisions per session

Every session offers exactly two decisions:
1. **Retracement trade** — enter on the retracement in the swing's direction (morning-session entry).
2. **Support-based trade** — after support forms, plan the buying side (overview-based entry).

Both can execute in the same session.

## Handling both sides — the two-position approach

### Structural distinction
- **First position** ← morning-session analysis (e.g., selling per morning swing).
- **Second position** ← overview/afternoon analysis (e.g., buying per major overview).
Different purposes, different analysis sources.

### Cut-and-hold system
Positions set on both sides: the side going AGAINST you gets cut; the side aligned with the major overview gets HELD. First slot cut ≈ 1:1 loss. Hold the overview-aligned position UNTIL NEAR THE LOWS/HIGHS — never early.

### When directions match — don't double
Overview AND morning both say buy → ONE position only. They clash (buy vs sell) → plan BOTH sides; the double-position approach applies only under conflict — without clash, single position suffices.

### Second position executes BEFORE first is cut
The opposite-side second position fires on the overview trigger BEFORE the losing first is formally cut (overview says: if rejection comes, short it). Execute second → then cut the clearly-losing first.

### Exact exit trigger for the buying position
Market starts falling with weakness and approaches intraday lows → the call/buying-side position exits there.

Sideways note: sideways days still pay the SOLD side — decay accumulates; at close, if the market shoots the other way, sufficient momentum arrives and you book it.

In the support strategy you never expect a rocket straight up — only a bounce. When a buying swing retraces and CAN'T regain the prior high (no recovery = lacking strength), either wait or act per what's taught.

Core point = PLACEMENT: observe momentum, exit the side going against you, hold the overview-aligned side.

## How V/W-shapes produce profit

The whole game runs on market nature: a first selling swing (sometimes 4–5 candles) is most likely to REVERSE (form V/W):

- Selling swing → reverses into V/W → traded → money.
- Next W also forms → continue → money again.
- No reversal (swing just continues) → STILL money, because the swing flowed in your direction throughout.

### Reversal probability degrades
First V-reversal after a selling swing = likely. After ANOTHER selling swing, another reversal becomes far less probable — successive reversals have diminishing probability; the first is the reliable one.

### Where does the loss happen?
Only one condition: dip → up → dip again without further breakdown → weakness confirmed but no follow-through → buying continuation runs instead. Otherwise sideways costs the seller nothing.

## M and W pattern identification

After buying swings → **M patterns** → selling opportunities.
After selling swings → **W patterns** → buying opportunities.
Losses come only when M continues down or W continues up — exceptions, not the rule.

## Stop-loss placement

SL at the **intraday low** (selling trades) or **intraday high** (buying trades). Don't weight a reversal all the way to the intraday extreme — if price travels that far, the overview was wrong anyway.

1. No execution before 9:45 — wait for settle.
2. Big SL + small quantity — NOT small SL.

8. Second (opposite) position fires BEFORE the first is cut, on the overview trigger.
9. Sideways pays the seller — decay works for you.
10. Aim: no session ends in overall loss.
